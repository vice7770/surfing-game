# P4d Rider on the Board: Design Record

> Built test-first on `claude/charming-sanderson-d9a2c0` after P4c. The implementation plan this record replaces is in the history of this file (`5a7c1f0`). It follows the [maneuver force note](../../research/surfing-maneuver-forces.md) (`ce2a6b6`): no trim, pump or nose-ride input, and rider load reaches the board only through contacts. Test code lives in the files listed.

**Goal:** A separate 73 kg rider on the P4c board (the [board and surfer plan](../../research/board-surfer-physics-plan.md) §3.2 and §5, B2). The rider lies prone and paddles, pops up by command, stands, shifts weight onto a rail, and falls into `main`'s `DetachedSurfer` when its contact fails. It runs in the worker and can be played in the physical mode.

## Sources (checked 2026-09-25)

- **Borgonovo-Santos et al. 2021** (laboratory pop-up):
  - 1.20 ± 0.19 s in total, about 60 % push and 40 % landing;
  - feet 0.63 m apart;
  - peak landing load 1.63 ± 0.18 body weights, 72/28 front/rear.

  These are a plausibility envelope, not tuned targets.
- **Shormann & in het Panhuis 2020:** the 73 kg intermediate rider, on a 25.9 L board.
- **Volschenk et al. 2021:** stroke rate and phases of prone paddling. The plan's 1.5–2 m/s sustained speed stays **provisional**.
- **Kimura & Kakinuma 2015:** a catch needs crest-relative speed and a place on the face. This informs the pop-up cue.

## Design

- **`riderPosture.ts`**
  - Postures of the detached surfer's seven parts; the tests assert the masses and volumes match.
  - The stance puts the rear foot 0.30 m from the tail and the front foot 0.63 m ahead.
  - Goofy mirrors regular across the stringer.
  - Centre-of-mass heights above the deck (modelling values):

    | Posture | Height | Notes |
    |---|---|---|
    | Standing | 0.85 m | |
    | Landing crouch | 0.57 m | trunk forward for a front-foot landing |
    | Push | 0.38 m | the arms do most of the lifting |
    | Prone | 0.12 m | chest arched, lying back so the nose just clears the water |

  - Standing and landing are **upright**: they are carried at the stance point on the deck and stay vertical while the board pitches and rolls under the ankles.
  - Prone and push lie **rigid** on the deck.
- **`AttachedRider.ts`: coupling**
  - The rider joins the board's implicit solve as one composite body. It is carried at the stance point, or rigidly lying down, and it pushes along a line through its own centre of mass, so weight shift torques the board.
  - The system is non-symmetric, so the board's 6 × 6 solve is now pivoted Gaussian elimination.
  - The contact impulse is checked, and projected when it fails:
    - it can only push;
    - its line must meet the deck inside the feet;
    - friction μ 0.9;
    - a cap of 4 body weights.
  - A projected contact solves the two apart, so the rider tips, slips or flies instead of receiving false grip.
  - Lying down, the body *holds* the board, up to 0.3 body weights of pull with friction to match. Its own buoyancy carries most of its weight, and without the hold, passing swell lifted it off.
  - Knee flex absorbs landings.
  - The rider separates when its posture error passes 0.25 m or a flight lasts 0.4 s. The cause is the limit that bound most in the last 0.2 s: balance, foot slip, lost board or impact.
- **Water on the rider**
  - Each part takes buoyancy and drag from the water it samples, and hands the water its reactions.
  - Lying down, drag along the body is sheltered to 10 %: the parts sit one behind another.
  - Paddling runs 60 strokes a minute for both arms. Each pull goes from 0.45 m ahead of the shoulders to the hip, beside the rail, with hand and forearm drag C_d 1.1 × 0.06 m².
  - The pull share is calibrated once (0.32) so flat-water paddling settles near 1.6 m/s. The hand then peaks near 4.4 m/s relative to the board, standing in for the hand lift and added mass that quasi-steady drag leaves out.
- **Balance and steering**
  - The body shifts its centre of mass to put the centre of pressure where the rider wants it. The shift is critically damped (0.15 s), and limited to 0.6 m/s and 3 m/s².
  - Across the board, it targets the middle of the support. Steering moves that target up to 0.1 m toward the requested rail: board +x is its left, the same for both stances.
  - Fore and aft, it only keeps the centre of pressure within 0.2 m of the stance middle. Trim is left free, as a later deliberate control.
- **Pop-up**
  - `popUp()` runs push (0.72 s) and landing (0.48 s) on minimum-jerk paths, then settles into the stance over 0.8 s.
  - Part positions are remapped when the frame changes between rigid and upright, so nothing jumps.
  - Standing requires three things; otherwise the rider lies back down:
    - the feet no more than 0.1 m under the surface;
    - the board sinking *into the surface* (not merely down a face) slower than 0.3 m/s;
    - the contact unstrained lately.
  - A cue shows when a pop-up would find the board planing down a face: planing pressure carrying 35 % of the weight, 2 m/s, and a surface falling at least 2° ahead.
- **`RideSession.ts`**
  - It owns the board, the attached rider and the detached surfer.
  - At separation the fall body starts from the rider's centre of mass, velocity and spin, moves on the same water through `SurfWaterBodyField`, and strikes the board through its contact seam.
- **Worker and view**
  - Advance requests carry paddle, pop-up, steer and retry. Presses count once per batch.
  - R (quick retry), or leaving the domain, relaunches board and rider in the lineup while the waves carry on.
  - Snapshots carry seven drawn points (hands and feet at their tips), the phase, the cue and the heading.
  - `PhysicalMode` draws `Surfer` via `updateDetached`, with its legacy board hidden, over the physical board. A ride camera follows the board, and the Wave Lab shows the rider, pop-up and fall rows.

## Verification

- **Tests: 323 in the suite, and the build passes.**
  - `riderPosture.test.ts` (4).
  - `AttachedRider.test.ts` (20) covers the contact on the tow and at rest, the separation cause, free fall, the energy ledger, prone floating, paddling speed and water momentum, the current, dry hands, flight, pop-up (tow, rest, strokes, cue, faces) and steering (direction, stance).
  - `RideSession.test.ts` (3).
  - The runner (+4), worker, host and `PhysicalMode` tests with a rider and live input.
- **Measured:**

  | Case | Result |
  |---|---|
  | Standing, towed at 6 m/s | Trim 8.4°. Contact 1.02 BW, 75 % on the front foot. |
  | Prone at rest | Nose up 4.4° with the nose tip at the surface, deck 12 cm under (a 0.35 L/kg board), head 26 cm above. |
  | Flat-water paddling | Settles at 1.57 m/s. |
  | Pop-up on the tow | Stands in 1.20 s. Landing peak 1.4–1.7 BW, front foot 0.6–0.8. |
  | Pop-up at rest | Fails ("no support") and lies back down. |
  | Pop-up on static faces | Stands at 15° and 18° (landing peaks 2.65 and 4.0 BW). Fails at 10–12°, where the prone board only reaches 3–4 m/s. |
  | Steering | Centre of pressure 0.03 m onto the requested rail, the board rolls toward it and drifts that way within 0.5 s. |
  | Separation | A board jammed still throws the rider over the nose; the fall body's momentum matches the rider's to 10⁻⁹. |
  | Real Beach waves (Hs 1.4 m, Tp 10 s) | Paddles 40 s without falling, surging 0.9–2.6 m/s as waves pass. 5.7 ms per step in total (bundled Node). |

- **Browser** (Beach, driven frame by frame through a temporary hook, since the pane was hidden):
  - the ride camera opens on the prone rider;
  - holding Space paddles to 2 m/s;
  - Enter pushes up, lands, finds no planing support, and lies back down;
  - R relaunches in the lineup.

## Deviations and open items

- **Beyond the plan:**
  - the upright standing model and the non-symmetric solve;
  - the prone hold;
  - the sheltered prone drag;
  - the second-order balance loop with its fore-aft trim freedom;
  - pop-up heights moved so no segment decelerates near g;
  - the stand check measured against the surface;
  - a cue share of 35 %.

  Each fixed a failure a test or probe exposed.
- **Catching is not yet possible on the physical waves.** Waves travel at about 4.4 m/s at the break; a 1.7 m/s paddler lets them pass, and whitewater only pushes it to 2.6 m/s. Catch calibration (plan §1.10) and a practice mode that keeps a rideable face are P4f.
- **Without fins the board has no directional stability.** The prone board wanders off its heading (about 45° in 20 s in oblique swell), and a board rolled onto a rail spins out after about a second. P4e supplies fins and rail grip, and prone steering with them.
- **Landing loads on faces** reach 2.6–4 BW, higher than on the tow (1.4–1.7). They should be rechecked once fins steady the board.
- **The fall body snaps its limbs at separation.** `DetachedSurfer.start` rebuilds its own standing layout around the handed-over centre of mass. A `startFromPose` in the surfer's own file would carry the posture; that is for the surfer's owner.
- **Worker budget:** 5.7 ms per step with board and rider (bundled Node), and 7–10 ms in the browser, against the 4 ms gate.
