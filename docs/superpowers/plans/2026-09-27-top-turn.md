# The Top Turn and the Cutback Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** A rider who climbs the face can turn back down it (a top turn, a snap in the lip, a cutback from the shoulder) the way the reference video and Forsyth et al. 2024 show, and the drawn body shows the snap: weight back, the trunk twisting, the arm up.

**Architecture:** First the physics. Measured before this plan on the 15° plane face (climbing 150° from the fall line at 6–8 m/s, then full steer back down):
- every top turn falls within 0.4–1.1 s, having turned only 6–51°;
- the board first yaws about 15° the wrong way (the lean's counter-steer);
- the body leans in past the lean the slowing board can hold, and past the rail's bite the feet stop braking it (the fall fixes' rule), so it falls into the turn;
- at 8 m/s the leg pulls the board (−6.6 body weights) and the speed collapses from 7.5 to 3.0 m/s in 0.4 s.

Each cause is diagnosed under superpowers:systematic-debugging and fixed with a failing test first, within the memory's don't-retry list (`p4e-carve-root-cause`). Then the snap's body cues go on Part B's rig. Then everything is checked against the video and Forsyth.

**Tech Stack:** TypeScript, Vitest, the carve lab and ride report scripts, the surfer sheet.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-27-stances.md` item 6: "the top turn and the cutback checked against the video (the snap: weight back, trunk twist, arm up)". Its references are the video ("Snap In The Lip Over Shallow Reef", Surfers of Bali, `xTgYU5ShueI`) and Forsyth et al. 2024's cutback and top turn: 152° in 0.96 s, 3.0 rad/s peak, 6.7 m/s, 2.2 m radius, 2.05 g, 75° rail.

## Review Focus

1. **A top turn with no weight back** (steer only) must still stay on when made at a speed the face can hold.
2. **A stall:** too slow at the top, the rider comes back down the face or falls honestly. It never hangs with the board sliding tail-first.
3. **Regular and Goofy, frontside and backside:** each works the same.
4. **The legs never pull the board** in any phase standing.
5. **The bottom turn's tests (the deep U, Compress mid-turn, the hard turn) stay green.** The top-turn fix must not trade them away.

## Findings

The study and every measurement are in `docs/research/rail-change-study.md` and the carve lab (`docs/research/carve-lab.md`, "Rail changes" and "Top turns and cutbacks").

**Task 1, the top turn stays on:**
- The fall had one root. The hull turns hard on a small roll (8° turns a held board at 0.84 rad/s at 7 m/s). The feet's counter-roll that threw the body into a new lean turned the board the wrong way (up to 1.75 rad/s), and that turn threw the body in at 3 rad/s until it lifted off its feet.
- The legs' pull, the fall past the bite and a governor on the bank asked for were each tried and ruled out.
- **The rule**, the user's first direction for the study, applies when all of these hold:
  - the rider steers past the heading hold's deadband;
  - the lean asked for is past upright;
  - the body lags it by more than the feet's linear range (about 2°).

  Then the feet never roll the board away from the lean; the upper body's swing throws it and the feet only catch it.
- **The first version was cut too wide.** It kept the feet from any lean asked for while steering. The final review found three consequences, fixed with tests that failed first:
  - a steady carve across the face gave its small steady rest to the swing, which holds no steady torque, so it wound to its range;
  - a held partial steer then turned the wrong way (+25° for −0.2 at 30° across) or not at all, and a tiny steer inside the heading hold banked to 70° and fell;
  - a stalling top turn, its decayed lean keeping its sign, toppled with the legs pulling up to 4 body weights.
- **Now:**
  - partial steer turns as it did before the plan, to within a degree, with the swing under 0.32 rad;
  - the legs never pull in a stall;
  - the 9 m/s steer envelope turns the steered way again.
- **Top turns from a straight climb** (a board placed climbing with the body upright) stay on and turn back past across the face within 1.5 s: 57–74° in 1.2 s, 100° by 1.5 s at 8 m/s.
- **A bottom turn carried up the face into the top turn still falls, every time** (pinned; 15 of 16 before the plan).
  - Carved up to 100–135° and steered back, the leaning body carries the board on to 160–180°. This happens whether released early or late, and with the weight back.
  - Top turns after a bottom turn will still fall in play. This is the cutback's and the deep U's root (below).
- **Rail changes** from a carve stay on at 6–10 m/s (they fell at 8 and 10 m/s).
- **Carves are smooth:** yaw rate 0.6–1.4 rad/s, not 0.5–2.35.
- **Compress over the crouch** stays on in the carve lab (it fell at 1.9 s).
- **Traded, against the plan's Review Focus 5. The user chose the smooth carve (2026-09-27)** (pinned, not tuned):
  - **The hard turn** makes 52° against its 60°. The committed 61° rode the feet's ~3 Hz pumping; the body leans the same either way.
  - **Bottom turns at the face's base:**

  - **The frontside reaching hand** is drawn 6.6 cm short (2.8 before): the swing throws the lean and turns the drawn chest about 5° out of the turn.
- The steer-only target of 120° in 1.2 s was beyond the lean cap on a slowing climb: a ruling, with the snap's 150° left to Task 2.

**Task 2, the snap and the cutback:**
- Weight back pivots the top turn at Forsyth's rates while the board planes: 2.9–4.1 rad/s, about 90° in the first second from 6.7 m/s.
- But the tail sinks, the board stalls climbing the static face, and the rider falls at 1.4 s with the weight fully back.
- No steer, weight or easing makes a cutback past 80–118°: the board slows below planing and the rider falls in at 1.3–1.7 s. Cutbacks fell the same before the rule.
- Both are pinned (`it.fails`) with the deep U. The carve sheds about 0.45 g and bogs past its 48° bite, where Forsyth's surfers hold 75°. A lab face that gives nothing back cannot show a top turn carried by the wave.
- That design step waits for the user.

**Task 3, the snap drawn:**
- The plan's cue, "the torso behind the pelvis", was wrong: weight back leans the drawn trunk further forward.
- The weight is read from where the pelvis sits over the stance, level. The body rides upright, so the read follows the trim:
  - level riding sits over the middle;
  - trim −0.5 sits 17 % of the stance behind it, trim −1 31 %.
- Climbing the face nose-up, the body sits back over the tail whatever the trim (−28 % at neutral).
- The first read was taken along the pitched board, so its pitch read as weight (the final review): a level carve down the face drew a third of a snap, and the real snap climbing drew none.
- From 5 % to 30 % behind, in a turn, the chest turns up to 30° further into it and the leading arm rises from 15° to 60°.
- The surfer sheet's `?riding` view gains the real rider's snap.
