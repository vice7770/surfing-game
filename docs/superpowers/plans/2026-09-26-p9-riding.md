# P9 Riding Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** A standing rider who trims, stalls, crouches, pumps and turns on the physical waves through physical inputs, measured against the wave and against real surfers, with a ride report and an optional score.

**Architecture:** The plan has two parts, because P8 (Menus and settings, `claude/p8-menus`) is being built in parallel and owns the game's UI shell.

- **Part A: physics and measurement (Tasks 1–12), now, in parallel with P8.** It touches no UI file P8 owns.
  - **Phase 0, measure first (Tasks 1–3).**
    - A wave-frame gauge measures the rider against the crest it rides: crest speed, the peel's required speed c / sin α, distance ahead of the crest, height on the face.
    - `status().ride.speed` becomes speed over ground, so every HUD, P8's included, shows the number GPS studies measure.
    - The default ride camera holds the horizon and stops lagging.
    - A line-holding autopilot rides for the recorder and a new ride report.

    This is where "the rider's speed and position relative to the wave looked wrong" is worked out, with numbers, before the rider changes.
  - **The flexible rider (Tasks 4–6).** Standing, the rider is a point mass on a leg of finite stiffness and damping.
    - The leg joins the board's implicit solve as a symmetric 9 × 9 system: board v and ω, plus the rider's velocity u. The leg acts at the rider's centre of mass, the coupling the P4e fix found to keep the solve symmetric.
    - The leg's rest line is fixed in the board frame. A slow balance reflex with a 0.1 s delay re-aims it, so the rider stays over the target centre of pressure.
    - Lean, trim and crouch move that target and the leg's rest length.
    - This replaces the rigidly carried upright body (the "infinitely strong ankle") and its one-substep drive lag, which the P4e analysis found behind the full-lock carve failure.
    - The prone, push and recover phases keep today's rigid composite, unchanged.
  - **Mechanics (Tasks 7–10):**
    - a wave-side hand in the water;
    - pumping, proved on a bumpy slope and on flat water;
    - per-step turn and ride-end detection;
    - an autopilot that turns.
  - **Scoring, body language and validation (Tasks 11–12):**
    - the score as a pure rubric of measured quantities;
    - the arms reaching out near a fall;
    - reports against the research ranges, with performance measured;
    - the recorder video.
- **Part B: player-facing (Tasks 13–16), after P8 merges into this branch's base.** It builds on P8's pieces instead of the Wave Lab, which P8 puts behind `devTools`:
  - the new keys as remappable actions in P8's `Bindings`, with gamepad analog;
  - the ride report and score on P8's end-of-ride card and Logbook;
  - `balanceReserve` measured on the flexible rider, for P8's balance display;
  - "Score rides" and "Balance meter" in P8's Settings → Gameplay;
  - one-time hints through P8's `strings.ts`.

  It ends with the playtest gate with the user.

**Tech Stack:** TypeScript, three.js math, Vitest, the stage 2 surf zone and its worker, rolldown report scripts.

## Global Constraints

- **Physics honest:** no scripted manoeuvres, speed bonuses or forces added to make a manoeuvre happen. Every mechanic is a rider input:
  - a centre-of-pressure target;
  - a leg length;
  - a hand in the water.

  A validation check that fails is investigated (superpowers:systematic-debugging) and recorded, never tuned into passing.
- **Values:**
  - **Body limits** (stiffness, damping, reflex delay, reach, rates) are design parameters. Their doc comment cites the study they start from and says `provisional`.

  ```ts
  export interface WaveFrame {
    valid: boolean;
    /** The wave's horizontal travel direction, unit. */
    directionX: number;
    directionZ: number;
    /** Metres from the crest to the rider along the travel direction: positive on the face ahead of it, negative on its back. */
    aheadOfCrest: number;
    /** The crest's speed along the travel direction, m/s, smoothed over 0.3 s. */
    crestSpeed: number;
    /** Crest height above the trough ahead of it, m. */
    faceHeight: number;
    /** Height on the face: 0 at the trough ahead, 1 at the crest (the water surface at the rider). */
    faceFraction: number;
    /** Breaking strength (the sample's `breaking`) at the crest near the rider: the most within ±4 m along the crest. */
    crestBreaking: number;
    /** Horizontal speed over ground, m/s, and its parts along the travel direction and along the crest (toward +x for a wave travelling +z). */
    speedOverGround: number;
    speedShoreward: number;
    speedAlongCrest: number;
    /** Speed needed to keep pace with the peel, c / sin α. */
    requiredSpeed: number;
  }
  export function requiredSpeed(crestSpeed: number, peelAngleDegrees: number): number;
  export class WaveFrameGauge {
    constructor(options?: { directionX?: number; directionZ?: number });
    update(water: SurfWater, position: Vec3Like, velocity: Vec3Like, dt: number, peelAngleDegrees: number): WaveFrame;
    reset(): void;
  }
  ```
  - `requiredSpeed` gives `Infinity` for α ≤ 0.5° (a close-out) and `crestSpeed` for α ≥ 90°.
  - `SurfZoneStatus.ride.wave: WaveFrame`.
  - `SurfZoneStatus.ride.speed` is now the horizontal speed over ground (what GPS studies measure), and `ride.boardSpeed` keeps the old |board velocity| for diagnostics.
  - Wave Lab readout rows (dev tools):
    - CREST: "c 5.1 m/s · need 7.2 m/s", with "close-out" for an infinite required speed;
    - FACE: "4.2 m ahead · 55 % up".
- Consumes: `SurfWater.sampleAt` and `surfaceAt`; `SurfZoneSimulation.peelEstimate().angleDegrees`.

**Method:**
- **Direction:** −∇η at the rider, when |∇η| > 0.02. On the back of a wave −∇η points seaward, so flip it whenever it opposes the previous direction. Blend toward the new direction with a 0.5 s time constant. Keep the previous direction when the water is flat. The initial direction comes from the constructor (the runner passes the sea's mean direction).
- **Crest:**
  - Sample η every 0.5 m along the direction, from 40 m behind the rider to 40 m ahead (161 samples).
  - The crest is the highest sample in [−40, +12] m. A maximum at either end of that range makes the frame invalid. Refine its position by a parabola through the maximum and its two neighbours, so the crest moves smoothly between samples.
  - The trough is the lowest sample from the crest to 40 m ahead of it (the samples beyond +40 m are taken as needed).
  - Valid needs a face height of at least 0.1 m.
- **Crest speed:**
  - It is the rate of change of the crest's world coordinate along the direction, (x, z)·d + s_crest, smoothed exponentially over 0.3 s.
  - The first measured difference seeds the smoothing, so there is no ramp up from 0.
  - A jump of more than 3 m in one update is a new crest: the smoothing restarts and the last speed is kept.
- `Vec3Like` is `{ x: number; y: number; z: number }`, declared locally in each file as the repo does (`V3` in `AttachedRider`, `Vec` in `BoardBody`).

```ts
import { describe, expect, it } from 'vitest';
import { exactWaveNumber } from '../wave/dispersion';
import { SwellWater } from './SwellWater';
import { WaveFrameGauge, requiredSpeed } from './waveFrame';

describe('wave frame', () => {
  const period = 10;
  const depth = 4;
  const k = exactWaveNumber((2 * Math.PI) / period, depth);
  const c = (2 * Math.PI) / period / k;
  const wavelength = (2 * Math.PI) / k;
  const still = { x: 0, y: 0, z: 0 };

  /** The gauge after `seconds` of a 1 m swell travelling +z, the rider held at z. */
  const settle = (z: number, seconds: number) => {
    const water = new SwellWater({ height: 1, period, depth });
    const gauge = new WaveFrameGauge();
    let frame = gauge.update(water, { x: 0, y: 0, z }, still, 1 / 60, 90);
    for (let s = 0; s < Math.round(seconds * 60); s += 1) {
      water.advance(1 / 60);
      frame = gauge.update(water, { x: 0, y: 0, z }, still, 1 / 60, 90);
    }
    return { frame, crestZ: c * water.time };
  };

  it('gives the speed a peel demands, c / sin α', () => {
    expect(requiredSpeed(4, 30)).toBeCloseTo(8, 9);
    expect(requiredSpeed(4, 90)).toBeCloseTo(4, 9);
    expect(requiredSpeed(4, 0)).toBe(Infinity);
  });

  it('tracks a linear crest at its phase speed', () => {
    const { frame } = settle(10, 1);
    expect(frame.valid).toBe(true);
    expect(frame.directionZ).toBeGreaterThan(0.99);
    expect(frame.crestSpeed / c).toBeGreaterThan(0.95);
    expect(frame.crestSpeed / c).toBeLessThan(1.05);
  });

  it('places the rider on the face: distance ahead of the crest and height on it', () => {
    // After 1 s the crest is at z = c; a rider 5 m ahead of it, a quarter wavelength ahead, and at the trough.
    const ahead = settle(c + 5, 1).frame;
    expect(ahead.aheadOfCrest).toBeGreaterThan(4.5);
    expect(ahead.aheadOfCrest).toBeLessThan(5.5);
    expect(settle(c + wavelength / 4, 1).frame.faceFraction).toBeCloseTo(0.5, 1);
    expect(settle(c + wavelength / 2 - 0.25, 1).frame.faceFraction).toBeLessThan(0.05);
    expect(settle(c + 0.1, 1).frame.faceFraction).toBeGreaterThan(0.95);
    expect(settle(c - 5, 1).frame.aheadOfCrest).toBeLessThan(-4.5);
  });

  it('splits the speed over ground along the wave and along the crest', () => {
    const water = new SwellWater({ height: 1, period, depth });
    const frame = new WaveFrameGauge().update(water, { x: 0, y: 0, z: 5 }, { x: 3, y: -2, z: 4 }, 1 / 60, 45);
    expect(frame.speedOverGround).toBeCloseTo(5, 6);
    expect(frame.speedShoreward).toBeCloseTo(4, 2);
    expect(frame.speedAlongCrest).toBeCloseTo(3, 2);
  });

  it('has no frame on flat water', () => {
    const flat = new SwellWater({ height: 0, period, depth });
    expect(new WaveFrameGauge().update(flat, still, still, 1 / 60, 90).valid).toBe(false);
  });
});
```

**Rubric (provisional weights, calibrated by the tests below; recalibrate against judged heats later):** WSL criteria mapped to measured quantities (survey §10).

- For each manoeuvre m:
  - difficulty(m) = 0.4·min(1.2, yaw/150°) + 0.3·min(1.3, peakYawRate/3 rad/s) + 0.3·min(1.2, speedIn/9 m/s);
  - commitment(m) = 0.5·faceFraction + 0.5·(pocket ? 1 : 0);
  - value(m) = difficulty(m)·(0.5 + 0.5·commitment(m)).
- The ride's parts:
  - manoeuvres = 1.5 × the sum of the three largest values;
  - variety = 0.5 × (distinct kinds, with top turn and snap counted as one) / 3;
  - combination = 0.75 × (linked pairs / max(1, manoeuvres − 1)), where a pair is linked when the gap is ≤ 2 s and speedOut/speedIn of the first ≥ 0.85;
  - flow = 0.75 × clamp((mean speedOut/speedIn − 0.7)/0.25, 0, 1), or 0 without turns;
  - length = 1.0 × min(1, duration / 20 s);
  - completion = 0.5 when the ride ended 'fell' within 1.5 s of a manoeuvre's end (a fall in the main section scores low, Peirão & dos Santos 2012), otherwise 1.
- score = clamp(round((manoeuvres + variety + combination + flow + length) × completion × 10) / 10, 0.1, 10).
