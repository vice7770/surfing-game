# G8 · Rich water

Agreed in a brainstorming session on 2026-09-26: approach 1 of 3, with every section approved. This is the requirements record; the plan follows it.

## Goal

Give the water the look of the user's references: *Surf World Series* (2017) in [gameplay at 6:36](https://youtu.be/TARlXzLDSBo?t=396) and in [a surfing-games retrospective at 5:33](https://youtu.be/ivd7SXxeaDI?t=333). The user put it as "not super realistic but with already a good graphic". The water stays drawn from the physics; this is a rendering change only.

What the references show, and this spec targets:
- a detailed, glossy wave face, with fine ripples catching sharp highlights and foam streaks stretched along the flow;
- heavy whitewater, a bright textured mass with mist rolling off it;
- a deep teal body, turquoise where crests are thin, with strong sunset and overcast contrast.

## Starting point

- **`WaterSurface`:**
  - one mesh at the 1 m render grid, lifted in the vertex shader by an RG float texture of height and foam per node;
  - normals from the grid's bilinear slopes;
  - `MeshPhysicalMaterial` at roughness 0.62, with G3 optics (Fresnel, absorption, the bed showing through, crest transmission), the G4 flow-mapped foam lace, and G5 caustics;
  - fine detail is a wind chop of six analytic waves, or the FFT sea on the GPU tier, fading within 80 m.
- **`FarFieldOcean`** carries the same chop and optics to the horizon.
- **`SprayPoints`** draws the G6 spray cloud (drops and mist) as soft points sized in metres.
- **Board physics** samples a Catmull-Rom surface over the same uniform render nodes (`PhysicalSurfWater`). The drawn surface is bilinear between 1 m vertices, so the two differ between nodes.
- **P8 graphics:**
  - presets are Low, Medium, High and Ultra, with an Advanced section (sea detail, caustics, spray and mist, ocean view, foam), applied through `resolveGraphics` and `SurfGame.applyGraphics`;
  - the Auto benchmark picks the preset.

## Decisions

### Scope and order

- **Priorities:** the wave face (A), then whitewater (C), with colour and light (D) tuned throughout.
- **Later spec:** the lip and tube look (B) gets its own spec once P7's tube physics resumes. P7's lip sheet is not touched here, because its data may change.
- **Out of scope:**
  - the beach, sand and coastline;
  - reflections of the surfer and lip in the water, and refraction;
  - any change to the physics.

### Approach

Approach 1, **layer detail onto today's surface**, was chosen over a camera-projected grid (a large rewrite) and hand-painted colour ramps (colour would stop coming from the optics).

- **One water, two looks.** `WaterSurface` stays the only water surface and keeps reading the same physics textures. A shader switch picks the look:
  - **Classic** compiles to exactly today's water, as the tested fallback;
  - **Rich** is the new look.

  `FarFieldOcean` takes the same switch for its detail and gloss.
- **The setting:**
  - **Water look: Classic / Rich**, in P8's Graphics › Advanced;
  - Low picks Classic; Medium, High and Ultra pick Rich;
  - it applies instantly (drawing only), and the existing foam, caustics, spray-and-mist and ocean-view toggles keep working with both looks;
  - a middle level waits for measurements that call for it.
- **Where the code lives.**
  - New shader pieces go under `src/scene/water/`.
  - Their textures are generated by script or in code, as the foam tile is today, so there are no downloads and no licences.
  - Edits to `main.ts`, `PhysicalMode.ts` and the settings files (shared with other work) stay to a few lines.

### The wave face (A)

- **The physics' own surface.** Rich draws the Catmull-Rom surface the board physics samples:
  - on a denser mesh, about 0.25 m spacing within about 60 m of the camera and 1 m beyond;
  - with per-pixel normals from that curve.

  Faces stop looking faceted, and the board and the drawn water agree between grid points.
- **Fine ripples.** Two tileable ripple normal textures add the small detail:
  - about 4 m and about 1.3 m repeat, generated from a wind-sea spectrum;
  - carried by the simulation's currents with the two-phase flow map the foam lace uses;
  - added to the existing chop (analytic, or FFT on the GPU tier) as its finest scale.
- **Where ripples show.** The face stays glassy where it is clean; ripples grow with the physics' foam value and a little with wind.
- **Gloss.**
  - Rich water's roughness is about 0.08, for a crisp glint path and highlights along the face.
  - Specular anti-aliasing raises roughness with distance and with the ripple normals' variance in a pixel (Toksvig-style), so the far water does not sparkle or flicker.
  - Crest transmission (G3) stays physical and reads sharper.
- **Face streaks.** Thin, faint, long foam lines stretched along the local current, only on steep faces, where there is foam or recent whitewater.
- **Far ocean.** The same ripple layers at lower strength, and the same gloss.

### Whitewater (C)

- **By freshness.** The physics' foam value decides what is drawn: fresh, high foam is dense **churn** (bright, clumpy, bumpy), opening continuously into the G4 lace as it falls. Both are generated textures carried by the currents.
- **Volume without moving the surface.** The churn's bulk comes from its own bump normals, darker creases and a matte finish (roughness about 0.7). It is not raised off the physics surface, so the board sits on what is drawn. Thin foam edges glow a little when backlit.
- **Mist and spray.**
  - The spray cloud's mist is drawn as larger soft sprites, lit by the sun with strong forward scattering (bright when backlit).
  - Drops stay small and bright.
  - Sprites fade near the water by sampling the wave height in their own shader (no depth pre-pass).
  - Particle counts and motion stay G6's.
- **Toggles.** Foam: Simple keeps the soft tint and Detailed gives churn and lace. Spray and mist: off hides the sprites.

### Colour and light (D)

- **Physical, tuned against the references.** Colour stays from the G3 optics: absorption with depth, per-spot clarity and the bed showing through. Only physically meaningful knobs are tuned, against the reference stills:
  - the body gain;
  - crest transmission strength;
  - the sky-reflection balance;
  - exposure.
- **Skies.** G7's photographed skies give the sunset and overcast contrast.

## Checking

- **Tests:**
  - Classic's shader source equals today's;
  - the drawn surface equals the physics' Catmull-Rom samples (a CPU mirror of the shader);
  - the ripple, churn and streak textures tile seamlessly and have their intended statistics;
  - specular anti-aliasing roughens monotonically with distance and ripple variance;
  - the setting's defaults and preset mapping, and that old saved settings without it load.
- **A water sheet** (dev page): a fixed surf zone (fixed seed, practice swell), paused on a breaking wave, rendered from fixed shots under the three skies, Classic beside Rich:
  - calm lineup water;
  - a close wave face;
  - a whitewater bore;
  - the far horizon;
  - the Below view.

  It is compared with the reference stills in chat; the stills are YouTube frames and stay out of the repository.
- **Performance:** frame time per look at each preset, measured on this machine (the M1 Air) with the browser pane shown (a hidden pane throttles), and recorded. It is never a gate. The Auto benchmark keeps weak machines on Low (Classic).
- **Done** is judged together through play, after the sheet.

## Order of work
