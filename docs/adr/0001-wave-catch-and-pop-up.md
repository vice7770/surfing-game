# Wave catch requires a timed pop-up and wave-driven ride

The current automatic crest-based catch does not match the intended surfing behavior. A wave catch is the sustained ride phase in which the wave carries the board and surfer; entering it requires the player to trigger a pop-up while board speed and local wave conditions are suitable. Paddling builds entry speed, but continued paddling must not be required to sustain the ride. This preserves the pop-up as a timing decision and makes wave-driven transport the defining MVP physics test. Readiness should be communicated primarily by visible wave/board cues, not a persistent tutorial prompt, and any forward ride must emerge from the simulated water/board interaction rather than scripted translation or staged animation; this is a hard product rule. Premature pop-ups are ignored; failure outcomes are miss or wipeout, while detailed failure recording is deferred to future scope. The incoming wave itself must be part of the evolving interactive water state from the start, not a separately rendered overlay that supplies motion to a disconnected board simulation. The first coupling pass is deliberately modest, with more complete fluid/board interaction, breaking, whitewater, and overhangs deferred to future physics work.
