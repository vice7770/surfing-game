# Surfing maneuvers: forces, controls, and feature boundaries

Status: design and implementation review for the physical-wave shortboard, 2026-09-25. This note distinguishes observed forces from theoretical explanations and game-model choices. It adds no speed, grip, or balance assistance to the physics. The current board reference is a 1.778 m, 25.75 L, 2.54 kg thruster; nose-riding belongs to a later longboard configuration.

## Force vocabulary

| Maneuver | What the surfer does | Force path the simulation must expose | Current branch and next gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trim / cruise** | Shifts their center of mass fore or aft until the board runs at a useful pitch and wetted length. The correct shift depends on the wave and speed; moving forward does not always reduce drag. | Gravity and foot loads create a pitch moment. Buoyancy and planing pressure respond through the hull patches; skin friction and spray losses oppose travel. A stable trim is a balance of forces and moments, not a board-flat rule. | P4c already resolves patch forces and pitch. P4d is adding a separate rider and center of pressure. Diagnose fore/aft load, pitch, wetted area, pressure and drag before adding a trim input. A deliberate fore/aft control remains later work. |
| **Carve / turn** | Moves load toward a rail, rolls the board, and sets a path across the face. | Lateral hydrodynamic forces from immersed rail and fins supply the acceleration toward the turn center; their application points also produce roll and yaw moments. “Centripetal force” names this net inward force, not an additional force term. | P4d rider contacts can produce a roll request. P4e must supply speed- and immersion-dependent rail/fin forces, stall and ventilation. Until then, physical-mode board yaw or roll alone must not count as a completed carve. |
| **Pump for speed** | Times compression and extension with motion up and down the wave face. | The rider changes board load and attitude while moving water does work on the board. Any net speed gain must appear in wave/board/rider work and momentum ledgers; a crouch cycle on flat, still water cannot create speed. | No physical-mode pump mechanic or extra input exists. Defer crouch/extension control until the shared board/rider ride works and the wave-energy path can be measured. Do not insert a direct forward thrust or a scripted speed bonus. |
| **Nose-ride** | Walks toward the nose of a suitable longboard while the tail remains supported on the wave. | Forward rider weight creates a nose-down moment that must be balanced by distributed water pressure, rail/fin forces and, when present, loading near the tail from the breaking lip. The contribution of each mechanism depends on board and wave conditions. | Out of scope for the current shortboard. A future longboard needs geometry, volume, walking contacts, stance progression, lip/tail loading and a moment ledger. Do not use a universal “lip lock” force. |

## Evidence and limits

- **Measured fin pressure:** [Kniesburges et al., *Scientific Reports* (2025)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-94834-0) instrumented one fin on a two-fin board while one surfer traversed a standing river wave. Pressure differed across the fin and changed through the traverse. The paper estimates a peak lift of about 300 N by assuming pressure uniform over the fin; that is an estimate, not a directly measured whole-fin force or a coefficient for this shortboard. It supports the direction and speed dependence of P4e fin loading, not a fixed turning impulse.
- **Theoretical wave-frame lift:** [Williams and Knight, *Acceleration in the frame of the wave* (2010 manuscript)](https://konfluence.org/media/fun/AJP_Surf.pdf) argues that rail/hull deflection of water rising along a wave face can do work on a board travelling across the face. Its idealized force and speed examples omit losses and do not resolve this game's board, fin or wave geometry. Use it to test whether board forces can draw energy from moving water; do not copy its thrust estimate into `BoardBody` or use it as evidence that pumping has a measured efficiency.
- **Nose-riding hypothesis:** [Landell-Mills, *Surfing explained by Newtonian physics* (2021 preprint, updated 2026)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/351914251_Surfing_explained_by_Newtonian_physics) proposes rail-following flow and a downward rear-board reaction as an explanation of nose-riding. The paper presents a mechanism, not a validated decomposition of tail, rail, fin and lip forces. The requested collapsing-lip counterbalance is likewise a possible condition, not a universal source of the balancing torque.

## Effect on work already underway

1. **P4c board:** Preserve its hydrostatic, planing, skin-friction and water-reaction force paths. Fore/aft and side-to-side rider loading should enter through contact force application points. The papers do not justify replacing the hull solver with a single “surf lift” term.
2. **P4d attached rider:** Preserve the independent rider mass, feasible support region, contact center of pressure, friction cone and separation. These are the foundation for trim and roll requests. The current P4d work has no pumping control and no reason to add one to its pop-up sequence. Record both stance and load direction so the same steering input can work for regular and goofy riders.
3. **P4e fins and rails:** This is where a physical carve can emerge. Check that lateral force reverses with sideslip, fades when the fin/rail dries, and changes trajectory only through the coupled force and torque. Keep measured fin pressure separate from game coefficients.
4. **Validation:** Compare matched fore/aft and left/right rider loads at the same wave seed and approach speed. Log pitch, roll, wetted area, pressure center, water-relative speed, contact center of pressure, fin/rail force, path curvature and work. A “trim” or “carve” label is valid only after the path and force traces show the maneuver. Pumping later needs a flat-water no-gain control case; nose-riding later needs a longboard moment balance.
5. **Worker cost:** The [P4c board record](../superpowers/plans/2026-09-25-p4c-board-body.md) reports a 6–7 ms step against the 4 ms target before P4d and P4e. Diagnose the cost of contact and fin samples before adding pump or longboard detail; no new maneuver rule should require a second water solve.

The current physical board has no fins or rail force yet, so P4d contact tests can prove that rider load reaches the board but cannot by themselves prove a physical carve. The legacy mode's turning feel is a gameplay reference; its direct yaw and simplified rail force are not acceptance evidence for P4d/P4e.
