# Surfer physics on the physical waves · P4 proposal

Status: **confirmed design; isolated surfer-kernel prototype started**, 2026-09-25. This plan records the surfer-specific decisions confirmed in Q24–Q41. It refines the [board and surfer plan](board-surfer-physics-plan.md) and [validation protocol](board-surfer-validation-protocol.md); no wave solver or playable mode has been changed. The implementation snapshot below is the active wave branch at `c3fce46`, not a claim about `main`.

## Goal and boundary

A fall must be a consequence of lost board support or an impact. The surfer leaves the board with continuous position, linear and angular motion; the wave, plunging lip, board and seabed then move and collide with a compact articulated body. After the forceful tumble, the player can swim, surface and physically rejoin a reachable board. Quick retry remains available throughout. The body and board use the *physical* surf-zone water in P4; the legacy wave and `RiderFall` are a baseline only.

This is a reduced physical model, not resolved 3D fluid dynamics or a medical injury simulation. Stage 1 water stores depth-averaged horizontal momentum. Orbital velocity with depth, vertical flow near a face and breaking turbulence must therefore be bounded reconstructions, identified in diagnostics. P5 may improve the wave but must not require a second surfer force law. One board and rider configuration is calibrated first; both stances use mirrored contacts. Leash, breath, injury, walking, individual fingers and anatomical muscle simulation remain outside this proposal.

## Body and force model

### Attached rider and separation

While prone, popping up and riding, use the existing plan's controlled rider center of mass, finite-response posture and hand/knee/foot contacts. The rider has its own mass and contact loads; foot forces move the board through its physical inertia. Steering requests weight shift, not board yaw. Loss of recoverable support, foot slip or an impact can detach the rider; a scalar balance display cannot cause detachment on its own.

At detachment, build the fall body from the *current visible/physical pose*: pelvis, torso, head and simplified arm/leg collision segments joined with limited waist, neck, shoulder and hip motion. This is a compact ragdoll, not a full skeleton. Distribute the configured rider mass and inertia among segments. Initialize each segment at its world transform with the attached rider's velocity at that point (`v_board + ω_board × r` plus posture motion), then reconcile total linear and angular momentum to the attached state. Remove attached rider loads from the board in the same step. Do not add a sideways/upward launch kick. A lip or board impulse may supply one if it really occurred.

The segment count, masses, volumes, drag areas, joint limits and constraint iterations are versioned configuration. Start with a small core-and-limb set; increase resolution only if a measured motion or collision gap remains. A constraint iteration or damping term must not add unlogged kinetic energy. The existing visual torso and two-part limbs follow the physical segments through joint/IK mapping during fall and swim. Blend their pose at separation, never their world position or velocity. Before separation, controlled animation and contact physics remain the authority.

### Water, collisions and swimming

At each major segment, sample the same-step water surface, bed, wet/dry state, depth-specific flow, breaking strength and lip contacts. Apply gravity, displaced-volume buoyancy and water-relative drag at that segment's center of pressure; off-center forces produce torque and tumble. Air drag can be coarse. No scripted spin, translation, surface clamp or fall timeout is allowed. Near a breaking bore, use a deterministic bounded turbulence/velocity-gradient proxy derived from the local solver fields; report its applied force separately. Keep submerged body points out of the seabed through contact resolution, not a free-surface floor. The board has independent hull/fin/rail dynamics and can strike or be struck by the body.

Direct lip contact uses swept tests so a parcel does not tunnel through a fast limb or board between steps. Because a lip parcel represents coarse water volume, collision uses a bounded struck fraction/effective contact mass, not the whole parcel as a rigid projectile. Exchange equal and opposite contact impulse with the struck parcel fraction, retain the parcel's modified momentum for landing, and test that contact plus landing does not create energy or count momentum twice. The bulk water's effect on the *surfer* is one way at this resolution; the board keeps the planned horizontal water reaction. Swimming strokes apply finite force against the sampled local water through the arm/leg contact points, with no direct velocity assignment. This one-way approximation must be labeled in the force ledger.

Swim control gains rise smoothly as impact energy, body angular speed and local breaking strength fall, with hysteresis so control does not flicker. There is no fixed unconscious timer. The existing paddle input requests strokes, left/right changes heading, and flotation plus strokes bring the head toward the surface. Use the same actions on touch controls. Immediate Quick retry is always possible. Near a drifting board, Enter/pop-up requests a grab only when hand reach, relative speed, orientation and contact support permit it; hand/chest constraints transition to prone contact without moving either body by teleport. A board can be unreachable and recovery is not guaranteed. On dry ground, board and body settle on the seabed; walking is not part of the loop.

## Simulation ownership and rendering

The P4 worker owns one simulation step index, inputs, solver state, body state, constraints and lip parcels. Define and test the exact order of water advance, body sampling, lip swept contact, body/board integration, reaction application and snapshot publication before tuning forces. Reactions may use an explicit split step if documented; no reaction is applied twice or from a render-interpolated pose. The render thread displays interpolated snapshots only. The camera follows the surfer through water entry and underwater recovery, keeping the board visible where practical; there is no forced cut or frozen wipeout screen.

An input/diagnostic snapshot includes step and seed; segment transforms and velocities; joint/contact impulses; local surface, bed and flow sample with reconstruction flags; board pose and forces; lip collision and remaining momentum; swim-control gain; board-grab constraints; window shift/domain status; and reset event. CPU runs replay bit-exact on the same build. All gameplay tiers use the same force and collision rules; visual detail may vary. If the total worker budget fails, profile water, sampling, constraints and lip separately, then simplify redundant body joints/samples and measure lost behavior before accepting the change.

### Independent kernel now available

`src/physics/DetachedSurfer.ts` is an isolated, solver-independent foundation. Its seven mass points use a `BodyWaterField` sample at each node, preserve attached center-of-mass and point velocities on launch, and integrate gravity, displaced-volume buoyancy, water-relative drag, seabed contact and force-driven swim strokes. Swim control is reduced by water-relative speed, a body-inertia estimate of angular speed and breaking strength, with separate entry/exit limits. Broad neck, shoulder and hip bounds use distance inequalities around the joints. A board contact seam transfers equal and opposite normal and friction impulses, with a swept check for a fast body crossing the thin board. A generic airborne-lip seam detects swept parcel/body contact, limits the participating fraction of coarse parcel mass, and changes the parcel's full velocity by the equal opposite impulse so later landing can use post-impact momentum. `BoardRecovery` accepts a near, slow, aligned grab and uses hand/chest spring impulses to reach a `prone-ready` handoff state; it releases a board that moves beyond reach. Controlled flat, opposing, following, sheared, breaking and outside-domain water fixtures exercise the body. The existing procedural `Surfer` and `CameraRig` accept the detached pose through separate methods; they are not called by legacy gameplay. This is **not S2/S3 completion**: collision uses a board pose held fixed across the body step, angle bounds are coarse, `prone-ready` is not yet transferred to the attached rider, and the actual P3b lip still needs a velocity/collision adapter, worker snapshots, runtime render binding and physical-wave calibration. Legacy gameplay continues to use `RiderFall`.

## Acceptance scenarios and failure signals

1. **Separation:** capture the final attached and first detached poses, linear/angular momentum and board load. They agree within the stated integration tolerance without an unexplained launch impulse.
2. **Water transport:** replay the same fall in flat, following, opposing and breaking flows. Segment force signs, angular response, submersion and resurface are logged. Dry samples produce no water lift; wet/dry and window-edge samples are finite and explicitly classified.
3. **Impact:** seeded board and lip strikes are detected by swept contact. Contact impulses remain bounded, parcel landing uses post-contact momentum, joints remain stable and the seabed is not crossed. Replaying the same seed and inputs produces the same trace.
4. **Agency:** compare strokes-off and strokes-on from the same settled state. Steering changes heading and strokes change path through forces; turbulence attenuates control smoothly. A reachable board can be grabbed and remounted with position/velocity continuity; an unreachable board does not home toward the swimmer.
5. **Playability:** preserve the [validation protocol](board-surfer-validation-protocol.md)'s player-controlled 30-second practice ride with bottom/top turns and assists-off record. Force a fall during it, follow the body underwater, then retry without stopping the wave. Report natural-set catch and ride distributions rather than guaranteeing each wave.
6. **Cost:** compare 1/60 and 1/120 s stability and measure full worker time, contact samples and constraint iterations on reference desktop and phone. A visual-only quality change may not change the deterministic CPU physics trace.

The surfing biomechanics sources support pop-up, posture and board-motion plausibility, **not** numerical coefficients for an articulated human inside a breaking wave. Body fluid coefficients, turbulence bounds and swim forces must be recorded as model parameters and calibrated against stable controlled cases and player-visible outcomes. Do not label them measured surfing constants.
