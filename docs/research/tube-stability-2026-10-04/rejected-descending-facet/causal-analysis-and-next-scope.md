# Frozen structural Hermite/facet trial: causal result and next scope

This is an offline rejected-as-complete candidate, not a production or native adoption. The exact recipe, prototype and exhaustive evaluator were frozen before measurements. No coefficient, per-frame gate, endpoint exception, or alternative construction was tried after seeing the result. Diagnostic reconstructions use that unchanged prototype for six selected failure receipts only.

## What this candidate establishes

The arbitrary crest-to-original-tip Hermite domain avoids the previous ellipse's negative floor-normal-depth failures. Every one of 1,224 raw frames and 3,648 prescribed quarter/mid/three-quarter adjacent-frame F32 samples constructs finite geometry. All original 32, 64, 88 and outside coordinates remain exact. There are no new zero edges or clean-to-crossed transitions in the authoritative **raw interpolation then joint construction** order. The 293 original held-eligible profiles have no increased or migrated crossing pairs.

The actual five saved outlines retain a visibly low descending lip, with crest-to-tip drops 1.569–2.030 m. Their 1.13 m continuous clearance widths improve from 0.382–0.578 m to 1.215–1.454 m. Four meet the declared 1.25 m width; row 124 misses by 0.03525 m. Every row misses the separate 1.6 m clearance over 1.25 m width target: widths are 0.533–0.816 m. Maximum gap 1.663–1.711 m does not substitute for that width requirement or establish board/body/path clearance.

Nominal paired sheet thickness is 0.240–0.250 m in captures. The nearest opposing-boundary measurement including attachment portions spans 0.240–0.537 m; useful 1.13 m intervals include vertical roof separation above 0.35 m. Thus the nominal thickness band passes, but the entire face attachment is not a uniformly thin sheet. The actual F32 active sheet stays inside all its roof half-planes. Frozen evaluator outside-margin counts (154 raw, 399 authoritative interpolation) are all collapsed zero-reach endpoints with floating residuals at most 3.5e-18 h0; those counts remain in the original report.

## Specific unresolved causes

1. **The retained floor is outside the roof construction's authority.** Reef frame 77 has a retained hump at 88–90 that rises through the falling 63–64 roof facet and returning inner branch. Two inherited crossing pairs become four, and the same count increases occur at shares 0.25 and 0.5 between frames 77–78. This is an inherited pathological floor domain made worse by this roof reconstruction; it is not a clean-to-crossed sample. The cap itself remains above the floor. Owning only 33–87 cannot smooth or lower the hump in 88–90.

2. **C1 is insufficient with a tight attachment and fixed sampling.** Captured attachment cubics have minimum radius 0.140–0.152 m, whereas an equal-arclength return edge represents 0.161–0.198 m. The near-vertical fixed throat compresses the direction change into one or two stored edges: captured turns remain 45–55° at 82/83 even though the exact endpoint tangent at 88 matches the retained face. Formation has 27 raw frames with newly introduced turns above 90°; Reef frame 128 reaches 145.46° at 87. A smaller tangent handle or more local samples would only address one symptom. A physically thick coherent ribbon also needs a curvature/thickness feasibility bound and adequate attachment length.

3. **The ordinary-wave domain is reconstructed without its own coherent phase.** All 876 R=0 frames have coincident L64=B88. This recipe collapses 65–87 to L and replaces the outer roof by a convex cubic. The ordinary surface can differ by 0.42244 h0 (2.957 m under the evaluator's illustrative 7 m scale). Zero-reach collapsed throat turns reach 100.95°. For a clockwise convex roof ending below the crest, a retained backward or upward face tangent cannot generally equal its forced descending terminal tangent at L=B. The recipe reflects backward tangents and limits the angle to its convex domain; those decisions are recorded, but they create a corner. They do not prove that *every* curve with these fixed positions is impossible: a nonconvex ordinary surface could have different tangent evolution. They prove that this globally convex construction with a collapsed, fixed throat cannot provide a smooth ordinary-to-curl lifecycle.

4. **Old terminal frames erase the curl by the source's topology.** Point30 frame 166 has reach 0.2976 h0; frame 167 has zero reach. At the 0.75 interpolation sample the new cap has penetrated the retained floor by 0.0003425 h0 (2.40 mm at illustrative 7 m), before the authored TD. The raw interpolation is already penetrated by 0.01016 h0, so this is not newly introduced contact from a clear source. However original64 cannot be the asserted impact landmark: the new cap extends below it, and its first impact must be derived from actual geometry. Other cases jump to a collapsed source frame at TD or earlier; a continuous game sealing model cannot treat that as a smooth geometric cap impact.

5. **Construction and interpolation do not commute.** Transforming frames first then blending produces 12 clean-to-crossed samples and 14 positive-reach sheet half-plane violations, including clean Pad30 155→156 at share 0.75. This after-query representation therefore cannot be inserted into the old once-at-library-parse transform route. It must evaluate a joint model after time/case parameter blending, with matching derived-state rules. The adjacent-time grid does not establish cross-case or 3D continuity.

6. **Envelope motion differs from the old material landmark.** Original64 XY and its sampled velocity remain exact in all 1,216 raw frame pairs. The largest whole-envelope velocity ratio is 5.27 at early Reef frames 2→3. Preserving one material point does not preserve surface velocity, flow, cap impact or useful opening lifetime. An explicit moving-geometry model must supply those channels separately.

## Minimum coherent broader change proposed next

Own **the whole 32–112 crest-to-toe contour**, retaining only the bulk-facing crest32 and toe112 positions/tangents where feasible. Allow old 64 and 88 positions to move and redefine their geometry semantics deliberately. The retained floor hump and compressed throat are inside this scope. Keep 0–31 and 113–127 initially so this is narrower than replacing the complete wave transport model. If the endpoint/tangent domain itself proves incompatible, report that domain and expand the outside joins; do not force reflected tangents or a per-frame raw fallback.

Use a small analytic C-profile as the game representation, with the library providing wave scale, speed, slope and broad break progression rather than supplying every old surface vertex as an immutable constraint. Construct one curved sheet and its paired discrete boundaries together; join its inner root to an analytic face/floor that reaches the toe with distributed C1/C2 control. Thickness and curvature must have a common feasibility bound so offset facets do not reverse or intersect. Allocate the existing 128-point budget by curvature/chord error across the structural parts; derive both sides from the same actual stored facets. A hard finite-budget failure must be reported rather than creating a narrow loop with a nominally smooth cubic.

Use explicit continuous **formation → open curl → sealing/impact → foam retirement** state. At zero formation the *same model* is an ordinary smooth face, without a collapsed L=B corner. Grow the rolled sheet by continuous model parameters, not independent old/new vertex morphs. The open phase carries a continuously moving hollow opening. During sealing, the actual low cap meets the analytic face/floor; compute that event from their separation. Authored TD may provide a broad phase reference but cannot assert that moved geometry has hit the floor. Once sealing starts, the same shared geometry closes continuously before retirement. No held-frame snap or source terminal R=0 jump should decide visible/contact topology.

Blend low-dimensional scale/phase/curve parameters across cases and alongshore, then evaluate the joint contour once. Do not blend two independently offset vertex arrays and assume they remain a ribbon. Keep monotone event age separate from a roof-coverage/opening channel. One authoritative query should return the contour and semantic landmarks/state used by drawing, contact, thickness, sky/void, loft and crash. Surface boundary velocity is the model derivative; material flow is a separate channel. If 64 becomes the actual cap extremum, refit/recompute its velocity and explicitly migrate every tip consumer. If 64 retains a material marker instead, publish a distinct cap/impact landmark and stop using material64 as geometric touchdown.

This is a **proposed scope/specification**, not another frozen recipe or evaluated candidate. Exact coefficients and a single complete construction must be declared before its next bounded evaluation. It intentionally removes the causes instead of retaining solver topology at all costs. A smaller analytic profile could also reduce runtime cost and particle dependence, but cost has not been measured and visual similarity remains to be demonstrated.

- Continuous gaps across the expected crouched 1.13 m footprint with at least 1.25 m width, plus the separate 1.6 m/1.25 m target. Include actual board plane, pose/tilt and swept body geometry; these scalar outlines alone do not demonstrate a rider fitting.
- Native moving exterior and rider views on the ordinary playable Padang Big case around 3.8 m / 18 s, through formation, entry, open travel, sealing and retirement. Show a descending rolling lip and maintained hollow passage, rather than a uniformly settling canopy. The earlier manual ride's balance fall with loft=0 supplies no clearance verdict.
- An ordinary full-physics paddle/pop-up/entry trial using the coherent startup route, with contact using exactly the visible surface. No geometry/pose teleport or forced covered state may manufacture entry. Record position/path, body/cap contacts, covered/loft transitions and reason for failure.
- Frame timing/profile-query cost at normal settings and movement; optional particle/foam detail must not become the geometry authority.
