# Water appearance and underwater view: WebGL2 decision note

**Recommendation:** retain `InteractiveWaterField` and its deformed `WaterSurface` mesh as the sole geometry and contact authority. Improve lighting in three steps: adjustable sun and sky with a refreshed environment map; optional low-resolution live reflections of nearby scene objects; and a camera-below-surface mode with distance tint plus decorative caustic-like light. None of these steps needs a second wave simulation.

Implementation update: the prototype has sun height/direction controls, a refreshed sky/coast PMREM after condition changes, moving field-derived reflection normals, an underwater camera with distance fog, and decorative seabed bands modulated by the field slope. A live rider reflection pass and physical refraction were evaluated and deferred because a planar capture would misregister on the steep wave face and add offscreen renders.

## Options and fit

| Technique | What it adds | Fit and cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing `MeshPhysicalMaterial` with a prefiltered sky/coast environment | Soft view-dependent reflections over the **actual deformed** mesh normals | Best default. The project already captures this map once; `PMREMGenerator` prefilters the environment for material roughness. Recapture only when sun/sky controls change, not every frame. `MeshPhysicalMaterial` itself has higher per-pixel cost than simpler materials. [Three.js PMREMGenerator](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/PMREMGenerator.html), [MeshPhysicalMaterial](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html) |
| Three.js `Water` addon | Planar mirror render, animated normal distortion, sun tint | Officially a **flat reflective water effect**. Its mirror camera renders the scene to an internal target during `onBeforeRender`; using it as a replacement surf mesh would make the visual wave and physics wave diverge. Its render-target resolution controls cost. [Three.js Water docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/Water.html), [Water source](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/examples/jsm/objects/Water.js) |
| Three.js `Water2` addon | Planar reflection **and** refraction textures | Its source creates both a `Reflector` and `Refractor` and invokes both before drawing water, so it adds offscreen passes. It is a useful reference for blend/Fresnel choices, but its planar capture does not solve a steep, moving crest. [Three.js Water2 source](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/examples/jsm/objects/Water2.js) |
| Screen-space/transmission look | Refracted view of below-water scene and an underwater waterline | Possible with render targets or `MeshPhysicalMaterial.transmission`, but transmission is a thin/volume material feature, not an underwater medium by itself. It adds material and render cost and needs testing at the moving surface boundary. [Three.js MeshPhysicalMaterial](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html) |

## Implementable path

1. **Sun and sky controls.** Add azimuth/elevation controls and derive one unit sun direction. Update the existing sun disc, directional light, and water material response from that direction. The official `Sky` addon exposes `sunPosition`, turbidity, Rayleigh, and Mie uniforms if the custom gradient sky is replaced; it works with `WebGLRenderer`. Keep the coastline stationary. Regenerate the prefiltered sky/coast map when a control is applied or after a short debounce, and dispose the old render target. For smooth slider dragging, let direct sunlight and the visible sky move immediately while the soft environment map catches up at a lower rate. [Three.js Sky docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/Sky.html), [Sky source](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/examples/jsm/objects/Sky.js), [PMREMGenerator](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/PMREMGenerator.html)
2. **Dynamic reflections only where they matter.** Keep the static/occasional environment capture for sky and coast. If the rider and lip must appear in water, prototype **one** low-resolution reflection render target, updated every second or third frame, excluding water itself to avoid recursion. Use the field-derived surface normal and view angle to distort/blend it on the existing mesh. A single planar mirror is an approximation for an irregular wave; reflections on the steep face will not be geometrically exact. Expose an appearance-quality toggle and compare real frame time before keeping the pass. The official `Water` source shows the mirror-camera/offscreen-render pattern and why it costs a scene render. [Three.js Water source](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/examples/jsm/objects/Water.js), [WebGLRenderer](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/WebGLRenderer.html)
3. **Camera-below-surface state.** Each frame, compare `camera.position.y` with `wave.heightAt(camera.position.x, camera.position.z)`, with a small hysteresis band near the waterline. When below, apply blue-green distance attenuation to scene objects, reduce contrast, and hide or tint distant sky/coastline. `FogExp2` is the inexpensive built-in starting point; its density is distance-based and it affects rendered objects, so coordinate `scene.background` and materials that opt out of fog. For a more controlled look, a small shader/postprocess can use `C_out = C_water + (C_scene - C_water) exp(-σ d)` with camera-to-fragment distance `d`. This equation is a proposed art-direction model; it is not a measured ocean attenuation coefficient. [Three.js FogExp2](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/FogExp2.html), [fog manual](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/fog.html), [MeshPhysicalMaterial attenuation properties](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html)
4. **Caustic-like light, visually anchored to the shared wave.** Modulate a shallow seabed patch and submerged board/surfer materials with moving, low-contrast bands derived from sampled field slopes plus sun direction. Fade with depth and break strength. This is a decorative approximation, not refracted light transport. The referenced `threejs-water` implementation is a Three.js port of Evan Wallace's pool-water demo and points to a separate caustics implementation; its tiled pool, enclosed walls, and separate water simulation do not transfer directly to open surf. [ThreeJS-water source](https://github.com/martinRenou/threejs-water), [Three.js ShaderMaterial](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/ShaderMaterial.html)

## Performance and authority checks

- The installed Three.js `WebGLRenderer` is WebGL2-only. Keep the current device-pixel-ratio cap and compare desktop **and** narrow/mobile views while the break, surfer, and UI are active. Record frame time, `renderer.info.render.calls`, and `renderer.info.memory.textures` before and after each pass; track render-target dimensions separately. [Three.js WebGLRenderer](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/WebGLRenderer.html)
- Avoid rebuilding a cube capture/PMREM every animation frame; `PMREMGenerator` renders and filters multiple directions, and a mirror/refraction target renders extra scene views. Dispose replaced render targets and textures. Start with 128–256 px targets, then raise only if the low-resolution result is visibly inadequate. These are **proposed test sizes**, not performance guarantees. [Three.js PMREMGenerator](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/PMREMGenerator.html), [Water docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/Water.html), [Water2 source](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/examples/jsm/objects/Water2.js)
- The rendered surface position, normal, and camera underwater test must always come from the same `InteractiveWaterField` state used by board physics. Reflection, refraction, fog, and caustics change pixels only. Treat a true underwater volume, physically correct refracted waterline, and wave-focused caustics as separate future rendering work, not properties of the current height field.
