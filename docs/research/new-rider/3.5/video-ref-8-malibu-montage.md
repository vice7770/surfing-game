### 0.7 — Slow motion: **2× and above are excluded; at or near real time is the best reading.**

- `playbackRate` is **1**. The delivered cadence is **exactly 1/60 s per frame** (31 samples at 1/120 s intervals
  across 5.40–5.65 fell into 15 groups of exactly 2 plus one singleton), and **every frame is distinct** — no
  duplication, so the stream is a true 60 fps. **That alone does not settle slow motion**, because a 120 fps capture
  slowed 2× also delivers 60 distinct fps.
- **The manoeuvre durations do settle it.** The whole bottom turn → climb → top turn in shot 4 runs **4.80 → 5.80 =
  1.00 s**; the snap in shot 7 runs **9.45 → 9.80 = 0.35 s**. Two independent populations put a real bottom turn at
  **0.96 ± 0.04 s** (n = 6, 835 turns, Forsyth et al. 2024) and a real cutback at **0.8 ± 0.03 s** (n = 4, 2,060
  cutbacks, Shormann & In Het Panhuis 2020), as quoted in `stance-frame.md` §6. **At 2× these would be a 0.5 s
  bottom-turn-plus-top-turn and a 0.18 s snap — both well under any published turn.** So 2× is out.
- A mild slowdown (≤ ~1.3×) cannot be excluded. **Best reading: at or near real time.**

## 1. Method and limits (read before any number)

### 1.1 Getting the pixels

- `yt-dlp`, `ffmpeg` and `youtube-dl` are **all absent** (verified). **Nothing was downloaded.**
- **A consent gate stands in front of the video.** The first navigation landed on `consent.youtube.com`, where the
  page has no `<video>` at all. I clicked **"Reject all"** — the privacy-preserving option — and the Short then
  played. **A later read should expect this and not mistake it for a dead clip.**
- **The resolution step from video 7 works and is worth everything.** The pane served **720×1280** after the consent
  redirect. Setting the viewport to **1280×1000 and then reloading** gave **1080×1920**, confirmed by
  `video.videoWidth/videoHeight` before a single measurement frame was taken.
- **Rider size in pixels**, measured as the height of the auto-detected wetsuit box in the 1080×1920 source:
  **96 px at the smallest (t = 7.00, a wide shot) to 616 px at the largest (t = 14.10)**, with a working range of
  **250–400 px** through most of the clip. **This is the second-sharpest footage the series has had**, after video 7's
  620 px.

### 1.2 Two tools that made this read possible, and should be reused

1. **An automatic rider crop.** Every surfer here wears a **full black wetsuit** on grey-teal water, so the rider is
   the largest near-black connected component in the frame. A 135×240 downsample, a luminance threshold swept over
   34–50, 4-neighbour connected components, and a plausibility filter on the box (height 90–700 px, width < 560 px)
   found the rider in **128 of 140 sampled frames**. The 12 misses are frames where the rider is behind the wave or
   inside whitewater, and the detector correctly returned nothing.
2. **An edge outline drawn over the silhouette.** The bodies are near-black against a dark wave face, and joints are
   invisible in the raw crop. Thresholding the drawn canvas and painting the mask boundary in yellow makes the
   thigh, the shin and the gap between them legible. **Every leg number in §0.5 was read off an outlined frame; none
   could have been read without it.**

### 1.3 The stale-frame trap — and a **second, different** failure this read found

**The video stale-frame trap was handled and fired only in a benign form.** Every drawn frame carries the reached
`currentTime`, a frame counter, the stream dimensions and the crop **stamped into the canvas**, and every montage
tile carries a hash of the frame it drew with a **`DUP!`** flag if two successive tiles hashed identically. The flag
fired **3 times**, all three on 1/60 s steps where the seek landed back on the same decoded frame — visible in red in
the image, and discarded as evidence.

**The new failure is in the canvas, not the video: the pane's compositor returns a PARTIALLY DRAWN canvas.** A
12-tile montage that the page had already finished drawing came back as a screenshot with **only the first 6 tiles
painted**. Taking a second screenshot, with no redraw of any kind, returned all 12. This happened **twice** before I
raised the wait.

- **It looks exactly like a video stale frame and it is not one** — the tiles that are missing are missing, not
  wrong, so the stamp cannot catch it.
- **The fix that worked: a `computer{action:"wait"}` of 2.2–2.5 s between the draw and the screenshot**, or a second
  screenshot. Video 7's 0.55 s was enough for a single small frame; it is **not** enough for a full-canvas montage.

### 1.4 What the error bar actually is, on this clip

**The hip landmark is the binding error, and these riders make it worse than video 7's.** Every surfer here wears a
**full black wetsuit**: no waistband, no gluteal fold, no skin, no colour change anywhere between the shoulder and the
ankle. There is nothing on the body to measure from.

**So I abandoned the shoulder-to-waistband scale the skill prescribes, and I am reporting why rather than quietly
substituting something.** On the same frame, three scales disagreed badly:

| frame | leg length by the **trunk scale** (shoulder→hip × 1.84) | leg length by the **head scale** (head-top→hip × 1.128) | leg length **measured directly** (hip→knee + knee→ankle) |
|---|---|---|---|
| t = 14.30 | 342 px | ~300 px | **226 px** |
| t = 14.70 | 342 px | — | **176 px** |

**The trunk scale over-reads the leg by about 50 % on this footage**, because in a black silhouette the "hip" is
placed at the bottom of the buttock mass rather than at the greater trochanter, which lengthens the trunk and
shortens the leg at the same time. **I did not average them and I did not pick the flattering one.**

**What I report instead is the leg chain's own extension:**

> **leg % = |hip → ankle| ÷ ( |hip → knee| + |knee → ankle| )**

This needs **no stature estimate and no size proxy**: the denominator is the rider's own straight leg, measured in
the same frame, in the same pixels. It is exactly the quantity the game's leg spring sets — the game's hip height
above the deck is its straight leg (0.88 m) minus the stance depth (`stance-frame.md` §1, quantities 2 and 3) — so it
converts to the ladder without a frame error.

**Its one bias, stated plainly: it is an upper bound.** Where the knee cannot be seen, the knee gets placed on the
hip–ankle line and the ratio reads 100 % by construction. **So every figure in §0.5 is "no deeper than this", and the
true depths are at or below what I report.** The frames where the knee was unmistakable (D3, D5) are the ones that
read lowest, which is the bias showing itself honestly.

**Camera elevation.** Shot 4 (4.4–7.1) is shot from **above and behind** the rider; at t = 4.80 the head-to-shoulder
distance exceeds the shoulder-to-hip distance, which for a human is impossible unless the trunk is foreshortened by
roughly 45 %. **Trunk angles from that shot are therefore over-read by up to 10 points** and are given as bands. The
remaining shots are near side-on from the beach.

**±15 points on every leg figure and ±10° on every trunk figure. I say it once and it applies to all of them.**

### 1.5 How the cuts were located

Two programmatic detectors were tried and **both failed, which is worth recording**: a 36×64 grey signature differenced
at 0.25 s gave 6–18 everywhere with no spike at a known cut, and a 64-bin RGB histogram with a chi-square distance
flagged only the whitewater explosion at 6.7–6.8. **Every shot in this clip is grey sky over teal water with white
foam and a black rider, so global colour and luminance cannot see a cut.** The cuts were found instead by stepping at
**1/60 s** across suspected boundaries and looking at the body: a cut shows as a framing jump plus a body
configuration that cannot be reached in 1/60 s. That is how 9.44 and 10.14 were proven and 2.84 was refuted.

### 1.6 How many riders

**At least three distinct boards**: a yellow deck with red rails (shots 1, 10), a plain bright yellow with a dark
tail logo (shots 4, 7), and a tan/orange with a dark stripe (shots 5, 8, 9, 11). All riders wear black wetsuits and
are shot from similar distances in the same flat light. **I cannot say whether this is one surfer on several boards,
or three or more surfers.** It is a montage either way, and every number below is therefore a figure about *surfers*,
not about *a* surfer — the same caveat `kirra-reference.md` carries.

### 1.7 Sampling and the tab

About **150 drawn frames**. Steps: **1.0 s** and **0.25 s** for the survey over 0–15.2; **0.2 s** for the
shot-by-shot pass; **0.1 s** through the four transitions (0.0–1.1, 4.7–7.0, 9.3–10.4, 12.6–13.7, 14.0–15.15);
**1/30 s** through the snap; **1/60 s** at three suspected cuts and for the cadence test. Magnifications 1.48–4.23×
on auto-centred crops, each carrying a **source-pixel grid** (20 or 40 px) so heights and angles were read off a
ruler rather than guessed.

**Browser tab: `tab-1`**, created with `tabs_create` and **passed explicitly on every `navigate`, `resize_window`,
`javascript_tool` and `computer` call.** The pane's pre-existing `seed` tab had to be used once, to open the pane
itself (`tabs_create` refuses to run when no pane exists); it holds the same URL and **no measurement came from it**.

### 2.2 The transitions — the centrepiece

**T1 — the unfold in trim (shot 1, 0.00 → 1.00, ≈ 1.0 s): the legs finish before the trunk starts.**

- **0.00–0.30 — the low.** Trunk near horizontal, head down over the nose, **both arms hanging straight down toward
  the deck.** Knees moderately bent.
- **0.30–0.50 — the legs.** The hips rise; the trunk does not move. It is still ~55–60° at 0.50.
- **0.50–0.80 — the gap.** Roughly **0.3 s of long legs under a trunk still folded 40–60°**, the head low and
  forward, the arms beginning to come out from under the chest.
- **0.80–1.00 — the trunk.** The trunk comes up to ~25°, **the arms open wide and level**, the head comes up last.

**This is video 7's T1 on a different rider: legs first, trunk about half a second later.** Video 7 measured the gap
at ~0.5 s with the rider at 620 px; here it is ~0.3 s at 250–300 px. **Two riders, two breaks, same order.**

**T2 — the fold-down (shot 11, 14.40 → 14.70, ≈ 0.3 s): the trunk goes alone.**

At 14.40 the trunk is ~35° and the legs read 97 %/86 %. At 14.70 the trunk is **~76°** and the leg still reads
**95 %**. The knees did not participate. The arms come down with the trunk and end hanging at the deck, where they
stay for ~0.5 s.

**This DISAGREES with video 7's T2**, which found the knees and the trunk folding together on the way down. Here the
down-transition is **trunk-only**. Two readings, two riders, two answers — so the honest statement is that **the
down-transition is not one fixed thing**, and the game should not be built as if it were.

**T3 — the turn (shot 4, 4.70 → 6.10, ≈ 1.4 s), in four beats.**

| beat | window | duration | what happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **entry** | 4.70–4.85 | 0.15 s | From a sprawled recovery out of the drop to standing. **The rear leg extends backward first**; the trunk stays folded ~65°; the arms are down and out. |
| **load** | 4.85–5.15 | 0.30 s | The body settles onto the rail; this is the lowest body of the clip. **The lead arm reaches forward and down past the rail toward the face.** Both feet disappear behind the board, which is why the depth is undecidable. |
| **peak** | 5.15–5.35 | 0.20 s | The board goes to near-vertical. **The rear arm swings above the shoulder while the body is still low** — the first thing to rise is an arm, not a hip. |
| **exit** | 5.35–5.60 | 0.25 s | The body extends up the face and then **folds over the board at the top**, and the lead arm is thrown forward and down across the body as the board comes round. |

Then the carve, 5.60–6.10 (0.5 s), which alternates: compress at 5.70, extend at 5.80, compress at 5.90, extend at
6.00–6.10. **Both arms stay wide and roughly level through all of it** — a different carriage from the turn that
preceded it, with the legs doing the same job.

**T4 — the snap (shot 7, 9.45 → 9.80, ≈ 0.35 s): the arm is up before the shot starts and stays up throughout.**
The rear arm is already overhead in the shot's first frame, the body stays coiled with the arm up for ~0.25 s, and
the unwind happens underneath it. **The arm is not a consequence of the turn; it is a held position the turn happens
inside.**

**T5 — pumping, and it is done with the trunk.** Shot 1 is **2.9 s of trim with no direction change** and **about
four fold/unfold half-cycles** in it, each ~0.6 s. At the one point in that shot where the leg chain is readable
(t = 2.00, in the middle of a fold) it is **99 %** — a straight leg. **Whatever is generating speed through that
sequence, it is not the leg ladder.**

**T6 — fore-and-aft, the axis the game does not have.** At 14.60–15.15, at 12.00 and at 10.40–11.20, the shoulders
travel forward over the front foot while the hips stay back, with the feet planted. The mirror appears at 0.50–0.80,
where the shoulders are far forward and the hips behind. **Three clips now report this — Kirra, video 7 and this
one** — and in none of them does a foot move to do it.

**T7 — what leads, summarised.**
- Coming up in trim: **hips → trunk → head**, arms last.
- Going down in trim: **trunk alone** (here) or **trunk + knees** (video 7). Not settled.
- Into a direction change: **the rear arm goes overhead first**, then the legs. 3 of 4.
- Out of a direction change: **the lead arm is thrown forward and down** across the body as the board comes round.

band, opposite arms. **So "tall ⇒ arms spread" and "deep ⇒ arms spread" are both refuted again**, and this clip adds
that the arm can change carriage completely while the leg chain moves by 4 points.

### 2.4 One thing these riders do that no earlier read has reported

**They ride with their hands near the deck.** Carriage A occurs in five separate windows and is the clip's single
most common arm position, and it is not the "hand drag" of a tube ride — the hands are over the **deck**, inside the
rails, usually **both at once**, with the trunk folded 60–80°. It shows up in trim (0.00, 14.60–15.15), driving into
foam (6.40–6.70) and recovering from whitewater (10.40–11.20). Video 5 and video 6's recurring pairing (lead hand
low, rear arm up and back) is carriage **B/D** here and is also present, but **A is more common in this clip than
anything else.**

## 3. Against the seven existing reads — leading with disagreement

**3.1 — DISAGREES with video 7 on the down-transition.** Video 7's T2 found knees and trunk folding together, within
its 0.3 s resolution. **This clip's T2 (14.40 → 14.70) is trunk-only: the trunk goes 35° → 76° while the leg chain
moves 97 % → 95 %.** Both are single observations on single riders. **The reading I would take forward is that the
down-transition has at least two forms and is not yet characterised**, not that either read is wrong.

**3.2 — AGREES with video 7's central finding, and strengthens it from four frames to a whole montage.** Video 7
found three of its four "crouches" measured 75–85 %. **Here every one of six readable frames is 76–99 %, including
the frame that looks most crouched of all (14.70, trunk near horizontal, leg 95 %).** Eight clips in, **the series'
middle-band numbers should be treated as suspect wherever the measurement was a hip height and not a leg chain.**

**3.3 — DISAGREES mildly with the first six reads on the top of the resting band, and AGREES with video 7.** The
earlier clips put the resting stance at 75–95 %. Video 7 saw 90–100 %. **This clip reaches 99 % twice** (D1, D4).
**The game's Tall at 89 % is not the ceiling for real riders**, and this is now two independent clips saying so.

**3.4 — CONFIRMS the fore-aft gap for the third time, on new riders** (§2.2, T6). Kirra reported it, video 7 located
it in the hips over planted feet, and this clip shows it in three separate shots.

**3.5 — ADDS a lead that none of the seven has: the arm leads the manoeuvre in time.** Videos 6 and 7 established
that arm carriage is *independent* of leg depth. **This clip times it**: in 3 of 4 resolvable direction changes the
rear arm is overhead **before** the legs extend (§0.3). That is a sequencing claim, and it is new.

**3.6 — CONTRADICTS nothing on turn duration, and narrows it.** This is the first read in the series with a complete,
unoccluded bottom-turn-to-top-turn sequence: **1.00 s** at the clip's own clock, against the literature's 0.96 ±
0.04 s for a bottom turn alone. **That is the first direct corroboration this project has that the footage it is
reading runs at roughly real time**, and it is what excludes a 2× slowdown here (§0.7). It also means
`kirra-reference.md`'s unverified 3–4× is not a property of surf clips in general.

**3.7 — Cannot settle the hold-versus-spike question, and says so.** The two deepest moments in the clip are
unmeasurable (§0.5): a loaded bottom turn hides the feet behind the board and a snap takes the trunk out of plane.
**Across readable frames, nothing sits below 76 %, so there is nothing to hold.** This clip's vote on duration is
abstention, for the third read running.

**3.8 — Does any rider sustain the game's shipped Crouch depth (66 %)? No — eight clips, still none.** The one
reading near it (13.10, ~66 %) is a single low-confidence landmark pick in a frame where the knees are partly behind
the board, and it is contradicted 0.2 s later by 13.30's 88 %/76 % on the same rider in the same turn. **The 0.195 m
rung still has no counterpart in any footage.**

**3.9 — No metre figure was inherited.** The centre-of-mass-for-hips error in `video-ref-2.md`, `video-ref-3.md` and
`kirra-reference-50-196s.md` is avoided by construction: this document names **rungs**, never metres, and §1.4 states
the denominator and the measurement.

## 6. Part C — what I could not see, and why

- **The feet in every loaded turn.** The board is between the camera and the rider's feet whenever he is on a rail,
  so **the deepest moments of the clip are exactly the ones that cannot be measured** (5.00, 5.50, 9.60). This is the
  most important limitation in the document, and it is structural rather than fixable with more pixels.
- **Knee angles as angles. I report none, deliberately.** I report the leg chain's extension ratio instead, and
  §1.4 says which way it is biased. Converting it to a knee angle would add a two-link assumption to a measurement
  that is already an upper bound.
- **Absolute hip height.** A full black wetsuit has no waistband, no gluteal fold and no skin between the shoulder
  and the ankle. The trunk scale and the direct leg measurement disagreed by ~50 % on the same frame (§1.4) and I did
  not reconcile them.
- **Most of the cut times.** Two cuts are proven at 1/60 s and one suspected cut was refuted; the rest of the shot
  boundaries in §0.1 are ±0.1 s from framing and board colour. **Global colour and luminance detectors both failed on
  this footage** (§1.5).
- **How many surfers.** At least three boards; whether that is one rider or several is not determinable from the
  footage (§1.6).
- **Each rider's stance (regular or goofy) and whether they are frontside or backside.** The riders are silhouettes
  in flat light and the camera sits near the beach at varying angles; I could not establish the facing reliably across
  the shots and will not guess it. (If the adjacent page text is right about the location, a point break would make
  them all the same way — but that text is not verified.)
- **Whether any hand touches the water** in carriage A, or only reaches toward the deck.
- **The board's rail angle**, and whether the board is on a rail at any given moment.
- **The rider at 2.95–3.20 and 7.10–8.10**, where he is behind the wave or inside whitewater.
- **The exact slow-motion factor** — bounded in §0.7, not measured. The page states nothing.
- **Footwork.** §0.6 records 0 steps seen, but the deck and both feet were rarely in frame together, so **this is a
  weaker "none seen" than video 7's, which tracked the front foot's position along the board across five frames.**
  Treat it as unverified here.
