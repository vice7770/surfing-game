## 1. Method and limits (read before any number)

### 1.1 Getting the pixels, and a resolution finding that matters for the next read

- `yt-dlp` and `ffmpeg` are **both absent** (verified). `swift` and AVFoundation **are** present and would have
  given deterministic frame extraction, so I tried to fetch the stream: only **itag 18 (360×640)** carried a usable
  URL in the player response, and fetching it with `curl` returned **403** because the URL is bound to the browser's
  own IP/session. **That route is a dead end on this machine; I did not pursue it further and nothing was
  downloaded.**
- **So: browser-pane screenshots, as the skill prescribes.** The video is drawn to an in-page canvas with the reached
  `currentTime`, a frame counter, the stream dimensions and the crop **stamped into the image**.
- **The resolution finding.** The pane served **360×640** at first. Video 6 reported that enlarging the pane gets
  720×1280; **enlarging alone did nothing here** — with the element at 509×904 CSS px the stream was still 360×640
  after 5 s of playback. **It took enlarging the viewport to 1280×1000 *and then reloading the page*, after which the
  stream was 1080×1920.** That is a sharpening of video 6's note and the single cheapest quality win available to the
  next read: **set the viewport first, then load the URL.**
- **Which numbers come from which stream.** The **survey, the timeline in §0.2, every duration and every
  pose description** come from the 360×640 pass (≈ 55 stills). **Every depth figure in §0.5 and §2.1 was re-measured
  on the 1080×1920 stream** (≈ 10 stills, magnified crops with a 20–30 source-pixel grid burned in).
- **Rider size in pixels.** In 1080×1920: **≈ 620 px** from face to sole at 9.00 s (skin bounding box 612→1228),
  **≈ 310 px** folded at 4.12 s — where she is also further away, about **0.70–0.74** of the 9.00 s scale by two
  independent proxies (board length, knee-to-sole). In the 360×640 pass the same rider is **100–120 px** in the first
  second and **180–210 px** from 5 s on. **This is the sharpest footage the series has had.**

### 1.2 The stale-frame trap, confirmed again, and what fixed it

**It is live in this pane and it survives a 1 s wait.** In the very first multi-step batch, a screenshot taken after
a seek to 1.50 s came back carrying the stamp `t=1.000 #3` — **the previous frame, returned silently.** It happened
**three more times**, always on the *first* screenshot after a crop or seek change inside a `browser_batch`.
**Every single one was caught by the stamp**, and discarded.

Two things worked, and the second is cheaper:

1. Two screenshots per draw, read the second (the skill's rule — reliable).
2. **A `computer{action:"wait", duration:0.55}` between the draw and a single screenshot** — after adding it, every
   stamp matched the requested time for the rest of the read. One image per frame instead of two.

**`tabs_context` reported the pane as hidden for the whole read, yet `document.hidden` was `false` and painting
worked.** So the app's "hidden" and the page's `document.hidden` are not the same thing here, and the stale frames
came from compositor timing, not from a frozen page.

### 1.3 What the error bar actually is, on this clip

**Above ~200 px the hip landmark is the binding error, and this rider's clothing makes it worse than usual.** She
wears a **one-piece suit with short legs**: it covers the iliac crest, the greater trochanter *and* the gluteal fold.
There is no waistband to use and no gluteal fold to see. **The only fixed marker on her body is the suit's hem across
the thigh**, and its offset from the hip joint is not a published number — I had to calibrate it **inside the clip**
(≈ 0.088 × stature, from the 9.00 s frame where her legs are visibly near-straight). **Every hip figure here is
±15 points. I say it once and it applies to all of them.**

**The camera sits behind and slightly above her for the whole clip**, which foreshortens a folded trunk and
**over-reads depth by up to 10 points**. That bias runs one way only, so where it matters I have said which way:
D1's raw read of 30–32 % can only be too deep, which is why §0.5 reports it as 35–50 %.

**Two methods, and where they disagreed.** Scaling from the acromion gives D5 ≈ 100 % and D2 ≈ 82 %. Scaling from
knee-to-sole gives a stature **30 % smaller** than the acromion method on the same frame — because on a wet,
near-straight leg the knee has no reliable crease even at 1080×1920. **I report the acromion numbers and I report the
disagreement; I did not pick a winner by averaging them.**

### 1.4 Stance and camera geometry, stated because everything left/right depends on it

The camera is **behind and slightly above**, on the face side. **We see her back** throughout (shoulder blades, the
line down the spine, the ponytail hanging behind, the back of the one-piece). The board's **nose points screen-left**
(pointed nose with logos at the left, traction pad and leash plug at the right) and she travels **screen-left**.
With her back to the camera, screen-left is her own left, so the **front foot is her left foot: regular stance.**
Her chest faces the wall behind her, so she is **frontside** for the whole ride.

### 2.2 The transitions — the centrepiece

**T1. Out of the tuck and up to full height: 3.80 → 5.40, ≈ 1.6 s. Legs lead, trunk follows ~0.5 s later.**

- **3.80–4.25 — the low.** Full tuck, arms narrow and low, lead hand on the deck. She arrives in this position
  already formed, out of the foam.
- **4.25–4.45 — the legs start.** Hips rise fast. The trunk does not move: it is still folded near horizontal. The
  **rear arm swings back and up** as the first visible thing above the waist.
- **4.45–4.70 — the legs finish.** Hips reach roughly their trim height. **The trunk is still folded 50–60°.**
- **4.70–5.10 — the gap.** Roughly **0.4–0.5 s of standing on near-extended legs with a trunk still folded forward**,
  head low, lead arm reaching down-forward past the rail. The weight is clearly forward over the front foot.
- **5.10–5.40 — the trunk.** The trunk comes upright, the arms open wide, the head comes up last.

**T2. Down into a crouch: 11.50 → 12.40, ≈ 0.9 s. Knees and trunk together.**
No leg-first phase. At 11.70 the knees are already bending *and* the trunk is already folding; by 12.10 both are
most of the way there; 12.40 is just more of the same. **The down-transition is simultaneous where the up-transition
was sequential.** The exit is not in the clip — she is still crouched when it ends.

**T3. The same shape, smaller, at 5.85 → 6.70 (≈ 0.85 s down and back up).** Knees and trunk go down together, hold
~0.4 s, and come up together. This is the clip's one *complete* crouch cycle, and it is **symmetric** — unlike T1,
which was the recovery from a position the barrel had put her in. That is a useful distinction: **the asymmetry in
T1 may belong to recovering from a tuck, not to crouching in general.**

**T4. The arms move on their own clock.** Between 10.04 and 10.40 the rear arm drops from behind her head to hanging
at her side — ~120° in 0.36 s — **while the legs and the trunk do not change at all.** The arm carriage in this clip
changes six times (§2.3) and the leg depth changes four times, and the two timings do not line up anywhere.

**T5. Fore-and-aft, which is the axis the game does not have.** Through 11.70–12.95 her **hips travel back toward the
rear foot while her shoulders travel forward over the front foot** — a counter-rotation in the fore-aft plane about
the hips, with **both feet planted**. The same thing happens in reverse at 4.70–5.10, where the shoulders are far
forward over the front foot and the hips are behind. **The fore-aft weight in this ride is moved by the hips over
stationary feet, not by stepping.**

**T6. What leads, summarised.** Coming up: **hips → trunk → head**, with the rear arm as an early passenger.
Going down: **knees + trunk together**, arms forward. In no transition do the shoulders lead the hips into a
direction change — but there is **no direction change in this clip**, so that is a "not applicable", not a "did not
happen".

## 3. Against the six existing reads — leading with disagreement

**3.1 — AGREES with clip 6 that arm carriage does not track leg depth, and strengthens it.** Clip 6 refuted
"tall ⇒ arms spread" with two Tall segments and two different arm patterns. **This clip has four arm carriages inside
the Tall band**, and the **lowest, narrowest arms at the clip's deepest leg position** (§2.3). After this, the
remaining half of the old synthesis — "arms spread implies tall" — also has nothing holding it up: C and F are both
Tall and only one of them is spread. **The honest statement is now that arm height and leg depth are independent.**

**3.2 — DISAGREES with how this series has been reading "crouch", and this is the finding I would act on.**
Three of the four lows in this clip *look* like crouches and measure **75–85 %, the low edge of Tall.** The depth is
in the **trunk**, not the legs. Clip 6 brushed this (its L1, "folded over the board", read 65–75 %); with 3× the
pixels it is now unmistakable. **A rider who looks crouched in a still frame is not evidence of leg depth**, and
several of the series' middle-band numbers may be measuring a folded trunk over long legs.

**3.3 — Cannot add to the "where in the turn does the low sit" tally, because there are no turns.** The count stands
at **8 of 9** from clips 1–6. This clip contributes **0 readable turns in 13 s**, which is itself worth recording:
**a professional-looking ride can contain no turn at all.** Its deepest low belongs to a tube stance and its shallow
lows belong to trim adjustments.

**3.4 — AGREES with clips 1–4 on the depth of a deep moment (35–50 %) and with clip 6's §4 reading that depth tracks
how hard the manoeuvre loads the legs.** The one tuck here is a **tube stance**; the one place she stands essentially
straight-legged is an **unloaded trim**. Nothing in between asks much of her legs, and nothing in between gets much.

**3.5 — DISAGREES mildly with all six on the top of the resting band.** Every earlier clip put the resting stance at
**75–95 %**. This rider reaches **90–100 %** for ~1.7 s (D5) — **essentially straight legs**, which is above the band
and above the game's Tall (89 %). It is a style pose on a long unloaded trim, not a working stance, but it means the
game's Tall is **not** the ceiling for real riders: there is room above it.

**3.6 — CONFIRMS the Kirra "not at all" gap (hips fore-aft) on a different rider, and locates it.** Kirra reported
the hips moving fore-aft over the tail and called it an axis the game does not model. Clip 6 could not see it.
**Here it is plainly visible** (§2.2, T5) — and the clip adds *how*: **the feet never move**, so the whole fore-aft
range comes from the hips travelling over planted feet while the shoulders counter-move. **0 steps in 13 s.**

**3.7 — Adds a transition asymmetry no earlier read has reported.** Legs-before-trunk coming up (0.5 s apart),
knees-and-trunk-together going down (§2.2). None of the six documents separates leg extension from trunk extension at
all, because none of them had to: this is the first clip with a long enough, sharp enough recovery to see them come
apart.

**3.8 — I did not inherit any metre figure.** The centre-of-mass-for-hips error in `video-ref-2.md`,
`video-ref-3.md` and `kirra-reference-50-196s.md` is avoided by construction: this document names **rungs**, never
metres, and states its denominator in §0.5 and §1.3.
