# Rider movement in clips 14–16

Clip 15 gives the clearest reference for the Canyon bottom turn. Twice, Mikey February folds low through the trough, reaches near the water and lengthens his legs as the board points up the face. Clip 16 shows a second use of compression. The rider bends and rises several times during one sustained run down the line. Give the player control over when to load and release the legs in both situations. Vicente judges speed carry and feel when he plays.

## What the footage shows

All timestamps are seconds on the published player's clock. I checked paused frames at the available resolution and revisited disputed postures out of order. The editing or slow-motion factor could not be verified for any clip. The board and rider can be compared within a frame. Camera motion, changing perspective and the lack of a calibrated length prevent estimates of speed relative to the water, force at a foot or hand, or hip travel in metres.

## Advice for the movement builders

| Observed movement | Where it maps to the game | How far the evidence goes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low trough entry, extension on the climb, then compression on redirect | Bottom turn and carve. `AttachedRider.prepareLeg` handles crouch, Compress, load-dependent flex and release. | Partly represented. Compare achieved leg extension, trunk angle, board path and speed at entry and on the climb separately when Vicente gives feedback. A taller silhouette alone cannot establish speed carry. |
| Repeated height changes with the feet planted on a board moving down the line | Rider-controlled pumping crouch. The pump probe toggles `rider.crouch` and holds `rider.steer = 0`. | Leg cycling is present in the code. Zero steer describes the input. The probe does not establish constant roll or heading, or how much speed a stroke adds. Preserve the quieter sections between rider inputs. |
| Hips and knees flex, the trunk folds, one hand reaches while the other stays high or trails | The standing body has leg and hip state. `HumanoidRig.followPump` contributes a downward arm bias as the pelvis rises. | Partial visual and physical mapping. The arm bias is one contributor. Varying arm carriage at the two crests in clip 14 and the reaching arm in clip 15 are reasons to inspect each hand independently. Footage does not establish a required hand force. |

## A defect in the pump probe

In Claude's pump research commit `ff750da4`, `docs/research/new-rider/3.5/pump.ts` initializes `firstPress` to `NaN` and sets it only when the key goes down. The `hold0` arm never presses the key. The common turning-point filter at lines 292–296 keeps only points whose time is at least `firstPress`. Every comparison with `NaN` is false. **The no-input arm therefore reports zero half-cycles for every signal fed to that filter.** Its published zero cannot demonstrate a quiet baseline. The positive-input arms have a first press and are unaffected by this specific defect.

I checked the exact `zigzag`, `halves` and filter expressions from that commit with eight 0.1 s samples alternating between 0 and 0.04 m. The detector found six turning points and five half-cycles. The existing no-press filter returned zero. Using the finite observation start retained all five. This was a signal-processing check without running the game. For the probe author, use the observation start for the no-press arm and check its measured result before citing a count for the baseline.

The positive-input benchmark distinguishes the requested leg depth, its smoothed rest length (`−leg.rest`), the achieved motion along the leg (`−leg.extension`) and the displayed pelvis depth over the deck. Under its 0.22 s command, the published pooled excursions are 0.078 m at rest length, 0.076 m achieved and 0.076 m displayed. The 0.45 s and 0.60 s command cases reach 82% and 93% of the 0.195 m rung in rest-length excursion. Those are properties of the bench. Mapping clip 16 to either command period would require real-time playback, actual endpoints and a treatment of unequal down/up times. Mapping to a target amplitude would also require a calibrated depth. Neither clip supplies a basis to change the leg spring or award a forward speed boost.
