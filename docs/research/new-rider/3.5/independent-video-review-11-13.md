# Canyon movement review · clips 11–13

These references give the movement team three different things to build for. A rider can redirect with one leg extended and the other tucked, change arm position while staying tall, and interrupt an otherwise quiet trim with a short extension. For Canyon, the owner's target is one bottom turn that carries speed into the climb. The useful distinction is between what the body does and what the board achieves. A convincing silhouette alone cannot tell us whether the board held its line or carried speed. The owner plays and judges that.

## What changes in each ride

Times are seconds on the published clip. Each clip had its own player tab. Frames were sought by the player's clock, captured twice and checked for a stable time, a distinct repaint and the correct rider. Samples included the start, interactions, recoveries and end of each edit. Unverified playback speed, camera motion and hidden joints preclude absolute speed, joint loads or a game timing constant.

## How that maps to the engine

The source baseline was `1a29a6c7`. The trunk and arm notes also cover the builders' opt-in branches.

For the owner’s Canyon bottom turn, follow the surfboard from the descent into the trough, through the curved section and into its first sustained climb. The banked board's change of heading should follow contact between the rider, board and moving water. The rider can lower the hips, fold at the trunk or reach before the nose visibly comes around. During the climb, the rider can release the turn while retaining enough travel along the face to continue. Lean, spray and a raised arm are useful visual cues. Speed through the water and the arc of the board belong to the board model, and the camera pan in these clips gives neither a calibrated speed nor a turn radius. ### Review of the trunk handoff

- The initial working diff chose the drawn trunk mode from the URL in `PhysicalMode`. Online setup cleared the rider and trunk options from the physics configuration. Those two routes could give different answers for the same rider. The rig should use that rider's effective physics state, including in a room.
- In the same diff, setting the requested visual hinge to zero still passed through the previous hinge, its follower and a rate limit. Coming into standing from a nonzero visual hinge could briefly apply the visual pelvis compensation on top of the physical fold. A transition can blend deliberately if its ownership is explicit.
- The substep constructed mass geometry before advancing the hip rotor. A comparison between those two angles needs matching time levels. A probe reading the rig's input points also stops upstream of the final skinned pose.
- The hard hip stop clears outward angular rate. Its internal angular impulse belongs on both sides of the constraint. Finite braking alone cannot guarantee that a moving limit is respected.
- In the arm branch at `bc5cf144`, `stepArmCarriage` fades reach over time but replaces the rail sign on **any** nonzero stick input. After a held turn, a tiny opposite input can flip the full-reach lead-hand target by approximately 0.44 m before downstream IK and filtering. A continuous signed target would let the arm sweep across the board during a rail change.
