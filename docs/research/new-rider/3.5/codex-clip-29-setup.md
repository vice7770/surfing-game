# Clip 29 · The first five seconds of the take-off

**Setup decision for the movement builder.** Start this reference with the rider prone on a board that already has speed on the face. Carry the board’s motion and the player’s chosen line through the pop-up. Let the rider look along the open face during the push, bring the feet into a nose-to-tail stagger, and emerge with free hands and enough knee bend to respond immediately. In this ride the open line is toward screen-right. A single ride cannot prescribe a default yaw angle, which foot must touch first, or an initial sideways speed. [Original clip, Hughie Vaughan](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/tqEm0RSmz6I)

| 1.65–3.03 s | The nose-side leg lengthens while the tail-side knee stays more bent. The nose lifts, exposing the underside around 2.0 s, then settles into a flatter-looking trim. The rider moves past successive fixed markings on the pool wall. This helps distinguish progress along the pool from the camera pan. |
| 3.25–5.00 s | The rider lowers into a deeper bend around 3.5 s, rises as the nose lifts by about 4.0 s, and flexes again. The arms open by different amounts. Toward 5.0 s foreground water hides much of the deck. There is no clearly visible step or loss-and-save in these frames. |

The black top and pale pink shorts make the waist easier to follow once the rider rises. The foot separation is visible mostly along the **nose–tail** axis. The **rail-to-rail** axis is foreshortened as the board turns, changes pitch and bank, and loses an edge in spray. The waist is above that moving plane and is a proxy for whole-body centre of mass. Neither reader could defend the sign or magnitude of rail-to-rail body speed here. The wall markings help with down-line travel. They do not supply a deck-width scale or the board’s changing orientation in three dimensions.

## What to compare in the game

- **Entry and line.** In the playable branch the normal launch starts prone, nose toward the beach. Prone steering changes the paddling action. Pressing pop-up starts the posture transition without resetting board heading or velocity. For this reference, compare the last prone heading and course with the first loaded stance and the first flexion after it. The footage supports a continuous moving entry. It does not isolate how much steer, water force or rail engagement produced the turn. [Launch](https://github.com/vice7770/surfing-game/blob/85fb6cb9/src/wave/SurfZoneRunner.ts#L913) · [Prone steering](https://github.com/vice7770/surfing-game/blob/85fb6cb9/src/physics/AttachedRider.ts#L4558) · [Pop-up input](https://github.com/vice7770/surfing-game/blob/85fb6cb9/src/physics/RideSession.ts#L461)
- **Support and pose.** The existing rig swings the front foot under the body and rotates the rear foot across the stringer. Both drawn feet reach their targets at 90% of the landing phase. At normal phase lengths, the physical feet support region is selected about 0.72 s after the press, the nominal visual touchdown is about 1.15 s, and the standing phase starts at 1.20 s. The solver determines contact load. Compare gaze, chest rise, foot visibility, actual support and subsequent flexion as separate landmarks. This clip supplies no physical load split, no mandate for simultaneous touchdown, and no clock for changing those phase lengths. [Feet and stance positions](https://github.com/vice7770/surfing-game/blob/85fb6cb9/src/physics/riderPosture.ts#L59) · [Phase changes](https://github.com/vice7770/surfing-game/blob/85fb6cb9/src/physics/AttachedRider.ts#L2905) · [Foot animation](https://github.com/vice7770/surfing-game/blob/85fb6cb9/src/scene/rig/HumanoidRig.ts#L1789) · [Touch setting](https://github.com/vice7770/surfing-game/blob/85fb6cb9/src/scene/rig/stagePoses.ts#L458)
- **Balance.** Track the board’s course separately from the rider moving across the deck. The balance calculation subtracts the board’s velocity at the support midpoint from the rider’s centre-of-mass velocity, rotates into deck coordinates, then projects out the component along the felt vertical. The capture-point calculation incorporates the resulting transverse component. The quoted 1.21 m/s was the median over 636 recorded falls across 53 settings, including gains that did not ship. It is neither a prescribed launch speed nor a hard-coded fall cutoff. Use this footage for stance and line. It offers no basis for an automatic recovery impulse or a change to the fall criterion.
