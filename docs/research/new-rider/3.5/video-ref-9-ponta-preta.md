### 0.1 — The headline: this is the clip where the LEG channel moves on its own, and the trunk channel moves on its own, **in the same thirty seconds, on two different riders**.

### 0.3 — The third finding, and the awkward one: **the leg chain is blind in broadside surf footage, and this clip is where that shows.**

The skill prescribes `leg % = |hip→ankle| ÷ (|hip→knee| + |knee→ankle|)` and warns it is an upper bound. **On this
clip it is not merely an upper bound; it is saturated.** Every frame I measured it on returned **96–100 %**,
including frames where the rider is visibly folded into the board.

The mechanism is structural and it is not about resolution:

> **A surfer's knees bend forward over the toes — perpendicular to the board's long axis. In shore-shot footage the
> board's long axis lies across the image. So the knee bend points at or away from the camera, the knee projects onto
> the hip–ankle line, the leg looks straight, and the ratio reads ~100 % no matter how deep the rider is.**

Measuring the same frames by a **vertical** quantity instead — hip above the deck, divided by the rider's own head
length, which no projection shortens — gives **30–66 %** on the same frames. **The two rulers disagree by about
thirty points and I could not close the gap**, so §1.4 reports both and names the bias of each, as the skill requires,
rather than picking a winner. **What this means for the series: the "every rider lives in the Tall band" result of
clips 1–8 may be partly an artefact of the ruler, and the "no rider has ever been measured at Crouch" puzzle has a
candidate mechanical explanation.** That is a claim about method, not about surfers, and it should be tested on a
clip filmed from in the water or down the line.

### 0.4 — The fourth finding: **a leg depth genuinely held, for about 0.6 s.**

The skill asks whether any rider **holds** a depth below ~65 % for more than a second; across eight clips none had.
**Rider B holds his deepest leg position from t ≈ 14.10 to t ≈ 14.70 — about 0.6 s — with the trunk upright
throughout** (§2.3, T2). That is still short of a second, so the question is not settled, but it is the longest hold
the series has recorded and the first that is unambiguously **leg** depth rather than a fold.

## 1. Method and limits (read before any number)

### 1.1 Getting the pixels — and the one thing that is different about a long-form video

- `yt-dlp` and `ffmpeg` are **absent** (verified). **Nothing was downloaded.**
- **There was no consent gate this time.** The first navigation went straight to the watch page with a `<video>`
  present. `video-ref-8` hit `consent.youtube.com` on a Short; a later read should still expect it and answer
  **"Reject all"**, but should not conclude the clip is dead when the gate does not appear.
- **The "enlarge the viewport and reload" step of videos 7 and 8 is NOT the best lever on a long-form video.** It
  got the pane from 720p to 1080p, but this video also publishes **1440p and 2160p**, and those are only reachable
  through the player's own API:
  `document.querySelector('#movie_player').setPlaybackQualityRange('hd1440','hd1440')`.
  `getAvailableQualityLevels()` lists what exists. **Check it on every long-form video; it is one call and it was
  worth a factor of two here.**
- **I settled on 1440p (2560×1440), deliberately.** At **2160p the pane could not keep up**: a play-and-collect test
  returned **two** `requestVideoFrameCallback`s one second apart (≈1 fps), and a 16-tile montage produced two
  silent duplicate frames. At 1440p every seek landed on the frame I asked for. **The extra sharpness above 1080p is
  marginal on this footage** — it looks like a soft or upscaled master — **but the extra pixels still buy landmark
  precision, and 1440p costs nothing in reliability.**
- **Rider height in pixels**, in the 2560×1440 source: the red-detector box runs **28 px** at the smallest (a
  distant wide shot at 12.20) to **392 px** at the largest (8.95), with a **working range of 150–300 px** through
  most of the clip. Comparable to video 8's 250–400 and below video 7's 620.

### 1.2 The screenshot ceiling, which nobody has written down yet

**The pane returns an 800×600 screenshot however large the emulated viewport is.** With the viewport at 1280×1000 the
whole 1280-wide page is squeezed into 800 px. I verified the mapping by drawing corner markers: **an overlay canvas
of 1020×760 is exactly the largest that fits inside the returned screenshot**, at a scale of 0.78.

**So 800 px is the hard ceiling on what any single screenshot can show**, and the only way to see more detail is to
**crop in source pixels and magnify**. Every measurement frame below is a crop of 170–370 source pixels drawn at
**2.2–3.7×** onto that canvas, with a **source-pixel grid** (10 or 20 px) drawn over it, so landmarks were read off
a ruler rather than guessed.

### 1.3 The two traps, and what each one did here

**The video stale-frame trap was handled and fired only benignly.** Every drawn frame carries the reached
`currentTime`, the presented `mediaTime`, a frame counter, the stream dimensions and the crop **stamped into the
canvas**, plus a hash of the drawn pixels with a red **`DUP!`** flag when two successive draws hash identically. The
flag fired on **3 occasions**, all of them steps smaller than 1/30 s that legitimately landed on the same decoded
frame, plus **two at 2160p that were real staleness** — which is what sent me back to 1440p. A `__seek` that waits
for the `seeked` event *and* `requestVideoFrameCallback`, then retries up to three times if `mediaTime` is more than
60 ms from the request, removed the rest.

**The partially-composited canvas is real and I hit it on the first montage**, exactly as `video-ref-8` describes:
the bottom row came back blank and a second screenshot with no redraw returned the same blank row — so **a second
screenshot does not always cure it**. In my case the "missing" tiles turned out to be **outside the 800×600
screenshot** (§1.2), not undrawn. **Both failures look identical in the image. Check the canvas fits before
concluding the compositor dropped tiles**, and keep the **2.2–2.5 s wait** regardless; it was enough for every
montage after the canvas was resized.

**A third trap, new, and it cost me a false conclusion before I caught it: an auto-crop that follows the rider
normalises his apparent size.** My first reading of the 14.50→15.23 strip was "a huge extension" — but the crop
widths across those tiles ran 340, 296, 289, 289, 282, 253, 267, 296, 311, 311, 310, 347 source pixels, so the rider
was being re-scaled by ±15 % between tiles. **A strip of auto-cropped tiles cannot be used to judge how tall a rider
is getting.** The extension in §0.1 is measured from absolute source-pixel landmarks on full-resolution frames, not
from the strip; and when I did that, the extension turned out to be **0.27 s later than the strip suggested**.

### 1.4 The two rulers, why they disagree, and what each one is worth

This clip has a **bare-chested rider with a visible waistband** (rider B, 12.15–21.45), which is the best hip
landmark the series has had — and the two rulers still disagree by thirty points.

**Ruler 1 — the leg chain** (`|hip→ankle| ÷ (|hip→knee| + |knee→ankle|)`), which the skill prescribes.
Readings: 96 %, 98 %, 100 %, 96–100 %, 100 %. **It never left the Tall band on any frame in this clip, including
7.300, where the rider is folded over his own board.** Its stated bias — an unseen knee reads 100 % — is not a mild
correction here; it is the whole measurement, because **a surfer's knee bends along the board and the board lies
across the image** (§0.3). It is also defeated a second way on this clip: **modern boardshorts reach the knee**, so
on rider B the knee is *inside* the dark shorts and cannot be placed at all in the deepest frames.

**Ruler 2 — the vertical hip height, scaled by the rider's own head.** hip-above-deck ÷ head-length, compared with a
standing human's trochanter at 4.08 head-lengths (or waistband at 4.62). **It needs no stature estimate and no
projection assumption for the quantity it measures**, because vertical distances in the image are vertical in the
world for a long lens shot from the beach. I cross-checked the head length against the rider's own trunk
(shoulder-to-waistband = 1.68 head-lengths standing) on four frames and they agreed within 10 %.

**Its bias, stated plainly: the head length is small and wet hair inflates it, and the result is linear in it.** A
15 % error in head length is 15 points of depth. The waistband-to-trochanter correction (0.07 × stature, i.e. 0.54
head-lengths) is another 5 points. **So ±15 points, and the absolute level is not to be trusted to a rung.**

**Where that leaves the depth numbers.** On frames where both rulers could be read, ruler 1 said 96–100 % and ruler 2
said 46–50 %. **I did not average them, I did not pick the flattering one, and I am naming no rungs.** What survives
is: (a) the **relative** motion within one shot, which both rulers and the apparent-stature figure agree on; and
(b) the **apparent stature** of §0.6, which needs no hip landmark and is the number I would build on.

**Camera.** A long lens from the beach, near side-on, roughly at the rider's own height or a little below. **There is
no behind-and-above shot in these 30 s**, so video 8's up-to-10-point over-read of a folded trunk does not apply
here; trunk angles are in-plane and I give them ±10°.

### 1.5 How the cuts were located, and what was not resolved

Global colour and luminance detectors were not attempted, because `video-ref-8` records that both failed on surf
footage and this footage has the same problem — grey sky, teal water, white foam everywhere. **Instead the two cuts
were found by wardrobe**: rider A wears a maroon top, rider B is bare-chested, and the two boards differ, so a
0.1 s sweep across each suspected boundary locates the cut to ±0.05 s. **Cut 1 lies between 12.10 and 12.20; cut 2
between 21.40 and 21.50.** A 0.25 s sweep across **24.45–27.20** found **no cut**, so shot 3 is at least 2.75 s
continuous.

**Not resolved: whether shot 1 (12.15 s) and shot 2 (9.30 s) are each a single unbroken ride.** Both are long enough
to hide a splice, both are plausible at a long point break, and in both the rider, board and wave evolve
continuously across every sample I took. **I did not prove it and nothing below depends on it** — each passage is
reported within its own window.

  chain, the same frames span **96–100 %**, which is §0.3's problem in one sentence.
- **Apparent stature**: **63 %–81 %** of standing height (§0.6). **Neither rider ever approaches a straight-legged
  stand in 30 s**, which is what the skill says to expect and is worth saying again.

### 2.3 The transitions — the centrepiece

**T1 — the trim pump (shot 1, 0.70 → 2.95): the trunk does it, and the legs are long throughout.**

Four fold/unfold half-cycles in 2.3 s, about **0.55 s each**: down 0.70→0.90, up 1.03→1.17, down 1.23→1.43, and on.
The trunk swings between about **27°** and **55°**. At the one point in the cycle where the leg chain is readable
(**1.367**, in the middle of a fold) it reads **100 %** — a straight leg. **Whatever is generating speed through
that passage, it is not the leg ladder.** This reproduces `video-ref-8`'s T5 almost exactly — four half-cycles,
~0.6 s each, straight legs — on a different rider at a different break.

**T2 — the compression cycle (shot 2, 13.77 → 15.23, ≈ 1.46 s): the legs do it, and the trunk does not move.**
This is the mirror of T1 and the most valuable single passage in the clip.

| beat | window | duration | what happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **down** | 13.77 → 14.10 | **0.33 s** | The hips drop and the knees come forward. **The trunk stays at ~30–35° and does not fold further.** The arms come down from shoulder height to waist height. |
| **hold** | 14.10 → 14.70 | **0.60 s** | The deepest leg position in the clip, **held**. The trunk comes *upright* during the hold (35° → under 10°) while the legs stay down. The hands go low and forward, both together, at 14.43. |
| **up** | **14.97 → 15.23** | **0.27 s** | **hip 1.77 → 2.82 head-lengths, a 59 % rise, with the trunk already vertical and staying there.** The arms spread wide and level as the body arrives, then drop. |

**Two things the engine should take from T2.** First, **the hold is a leg-depth hold, not a pose** — 0.6 s of a
depth the game can only reach by a stance change. Second, **the trunk un-folds while the legs stay down**: for
0.6 s the rider is deep in the legs and upright in the trunk at the same time, which is a posture a single
leg-spring cannot form.

**T3 — the down-transition, a third answer.** The open disagreement is video 7 (knees and trunk together, 0.3 s)
against video 8 (trunk only). **This clip gives a third form: legs only.** T2's "down" beat drops the hips over
0.33 s with the trunk angle unchanged within my ±10°. Rider A's fold at 0.70→0.90, by contrast, is **trunk-dominant
with some knee**, closer to video 7's reading. **So the down-transition now has three observed forms across three
clips, and the reading that it is not one fixed thing is strengthened, not settled.** The engine should not be built
as if it were one thing.

**T4 — the direction change, in four beats (shot 2, 17.97 → 18.80, ≈ 0.83 s), and the arm is beat zero.**

| beat | window | duration | what happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **arm** | **18.00 → 18.07** | **0.07 s** | **The rear arm rises from chest height to above the head. Nothing else has changed: the board is still flat and trimming.** |
| **entry** | 18.07 → 18.13 | 0.06 s | The trunk begins to fold toward the nose, the lead arm reaches down-left, the rail bites, the first spray appears at the tail. |
| **load** | 18.13 → 18.40 | 0.27 s | The body compresses onto the rail, the trunk folds through 40–60°, the spray fans. **The rear arm has not moved — it is parked overhead.** |
| **exit** | 18.40 → 18.80 | 0.40 s | The board comes round under a trunk folded 60–70°, the lead arm stays low near the deck, and **the rear arm is still overhead at 18.80**. |

**The arm is not a consequence of the turn; it is a held position that the turn happens inside** — `video-ref-8`'s
sentence, now with a 1/30 s timestamp on it. Rider A does the same thing more slowly: the rear arm goes above the
head at **7.63**, the body's rotation begins at **7.90**, a **0.3 s** lead, and the hand stays high through 7.83.

**T5 — the roundhouse cutback (shot 1, 8.00 → 9.10, ≈ 1.10 s), which is the exception on arms.**
Entry 8.00–8.15 with the **lead arm reaching forward and down past the rail**; the rail engages and the tail throws
spray 8.20–8.50; the board comes round 8.60–9.10. **Both arms stay low and roughly level through all of it — no
overhead arm.** So the overhead arm is not universal: it appeared in 2 of 2 changes I could resolve at ≥15 fps, but
this slower, bigger-radius turn is carried with the lead arm instead. **Two arm patterns for two kinds of turn is
itself a finding, and the game has neither.**

**T6 — fore-and-aft, the axis the game does not have.** At **7.17–7.57** (rider A) the shoulders travel forward over
the front foot while the hips stay back toward the tail, feet planted; the same at **14.30–14.43** (rider B, hips
back, hands reaching forward) and at **1.23–1.43** (rider A, shoulders out over the nose, hips behind).
**Four clips now report this — Kirra, video 7, video 8 and this one — and in none of them does a foot move to do
it.** My footwork count here is **0 steps in ~3.5 s** of frames where the feet and the deck were both legible, which
is a weaker "none seen" than video 7's but stronger than video 8's.

**T7 — what leads, summarised for this clip.**
- **Into a direction change:** **the rear arm goes overhead first**, by 0.07–0.30 s. 2 of 2 resolved. One slower
  cutback instead leads with **the lead arm reaching forward and down past the rail**.
- **Down into a compression:** **legs alone** (rider B) or **trunk with some knee** (rider A). Not one thing.
- **Up out of a compression:** **legs alone, trunk already upright and staying so** (rider B, 0.27 s), with the
  **arms spreading wide as the body arrives**, not before.
- **Through trim:** **trunk alone**, four half-cycles of ~0.55 s over straight legs (rider A).
- **Out of a direction change:** the trunk is still folded 60–70° a full 0.4 s after the board has come round.

| # | carriage | windows | count |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **both hands low and forward, near the deck**, trunk folded | 0.00–0.30, 7.17–7.57, 9.70, 10.95, 14.43, 21.20 | **6** |

**Carriage is independent of depth, again.** The decisive pair is in one shot, 0.5 s apart: **t = 14.70, the deepest

and **t = 7.30, straight legs, both hands low** — the opposite pairing. **Both "tall ⇒ arms spread" and "deep ⇒ arms
spread" stay refuted**, for the third clip running. What this clip adds is that carriage **D is a held position with
a duration** (0.7 s at 18.03–18.80, 0.2 s at 7.63–7.83), not an instant.

### 2.5 One thing these riders do that the earlier reads have not reported

**They drive down the line folded almost in half, with straight legs, for nearly half a second at a time.** Rider A
at **7.17–7.57** has his trunk at 50–60°, his head out over the nose, his leg chain at **96 %**, and he holds it for
**0.40 s** while the board runs flat and fast. It is not a crouch, it is not a bottom turn, and it is not a pump —
it is a **sustained aerodynamic-looking fold over long legs**, and it is how both riders cover ground between
manoeuvres. Rider B does the same at 17.20 (47°) and 26.70 (60–70°). **In the game this posture does not exist in
any stance, at any depth.**

## 3. Against the eight existing reads — leading with disagreement

image**, so the bend is invisible in projection (§0.3). **This is not a reason to go back to the waistband scale.**
It is a reason to treat "the rider is in the Tall band" as **untested wherever it rests on a leg chain measured in
broadside footage**, which is clips 7, 8 and 9. **A clip shot from the water, or down the line, would settle it.**

**3.3 — DISAGREES with the series' "no rider ever holds a depth" finding, mildly.** Across eight clips, none held a
