# Kirra reference clip: what the rider's body does

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3nfW6OLiyYw, "Kelly Slater's Iconic session at Kirra | Slowed Down", first 50 s of the main video (after two ads; the clip is 195.6 s). The owner chose it as the movement target and said to ignore the wave.

## Method and limits (read first)

- I cannot watch video. I seeked the player to fixed times (video clock, `video.currentTime`, slowed footage so these are slowed-down seconds, not real seconds) and read still frames. 17 frames at 2.5 s steps from 0 to 50 s, plus 6 close crops between 38.5 and 46 s.
- Source is 854x480. The rider is 40-90 px tall in frame, so everything below is **an estimate from pixels**, never a measurement. Joint angles are good to roughly +-15 degrees at best.
- The footage is a montage. It cuts between at least three rides: a long tube ride (0-~20 s), then an exit/wipe-out-in-foam shot (~20-26 s, no rider visible at 22.5 to 27.5 s), then an open-face section (~28-47 s) of turns. Only the first and third give usable body detail.
- Between my frames (2.5 s apart) a lot happens in slowed footage. I cannot say how fast any transition is from the coarse frames; the 38.5-41.7 s crops (0.7-1.6 s apart) are the only fine data.
- Camera is mostly a side-on or slightly elevated three-quarter view from the beach side, so the rider's front/back lean is visible and left/right lean is not. From 38 to 45 s the camera is higher and closer.

## 1. Stance through the ride (estimates)

Tube section, 0-17.5 s (6 frames):
- Almost always a low crouch. Knees bent roughly 90-110 degrees (thigh near horizontal or a little above), trunk pitched forward 30-45 degrees, hips low and back over the tail. Estimated hip height about 50-60% of standing hip height.
- At 0 s he is deepest (looks like knee ~100, torso nearly folded onto the front thigh). At 7.5-12.5 s he is a little taller, knee ~110-120, hip height about 60-70%, but still clearly crouched. He does not stand fully up at any sampled frame in the tube.
- Stance is wide: front foot forward of the board's centre, back foot near the tail. Weight sits mostly on the back foot in the tube (the trunk leans forward, the hips sit back).

Open-face section, 28-47 s:
- 32.5 s: tall. Knees about 140-150 degrees (only slightly bent), hip height about 80-85%, trunk fairly upright, one arm extended forward.
- 35 s: knees ~120-130, hip ~70-75%, upright trunk.
- 38.5 s: much lower and back on the tail, knees ~90-100, hips behind the back foot (the board's nose is high, side-on view).
- 40 s, 40.8 s: still low, knees ~100-110, trunk twisted so shoulders face down the face.
- 41.7 s: back up, knees ~130-140, hip ~75-80%, trunk upright.
- He therefore does go down and up, not stay low: the cycle (tall, low, tall) repeated in the turn section. Hip height estimated range 60-85% across the section.

## 2. What the body does in a turn (open-face section, estimates)

Read from the 37.5-41.7 s sequence (one bottom-to-top style turn and a carve after it):
- Entry (35-37.5 s): he gets taller and moves a little forward, shoulders roughly square to the board's travel, the board still flat or lightly on its rail.
- Loading (38.5-39.2 s): hips drop and sit back over the tail, trunk turns so the chest faces the open face (down the wave), the lead shoulder leads the rotation. The board noses up and the tail digs; the spray sheet is already coming off the tail. In these frames the shoulders and the hips are visibly rotated differently: the upper body is turned further toward the direction of the turn than the lower body. I read this as upper body leading, board following, but at this frame spacing I cannot be sure of order.
- Peak (40-40.8 s): maximum board tilt. He is crouched, hips low, inside shoulder low, trunk leaning toward the inside of the turn, head up and looking down the line. Weight on the back foot and on the rail side.
- Exit (41.7 s): he straightens, trunk upright and over the board, back foot still on the tail, board flat again.
- Later (43.7-46 s) he goes vertical on the lip: board near upright, body above and behind it, a head-first lean toward the face. This is an aerial or a vertical hit; the frames are too few to say how it started or ended.

## 3. Arms

- Tube: both hands low, close to the front knee and thigh, one hand near the front thigh and one trailing toward the wall. Arms are bent, not extended, doing a balancing/stabilising role. (Visible at 5, 7.5, 12.5 s.)
- Open face turns: arms spread wide at 38.5-41.7 s, one forward along the board and one back, roughly in line with the shoulders, level at about waist to chest height. The lead arm points toward the turn direction. They move together with the trunk rotation (the arm tracks the shoulder line), not independently.
- At the top (43.7-46 s) one arm reaches up and away, the other down toward the board. I cannot tell whether this is a balance reflex or a deliberate counter-rotation.

## 4. Timing (video clock, slowed footage)

- The slowed video is not real time; I could not find the slow-down factor in the page, so I do not convert to real seconds.
- A single turn from entry to exit lasts about 3-4 video seconds (35 -> 41.7 s). The low loaded phase is about 1.5-2 video seconds (38.5-40.8 s). The tube crouch lasts at least 15 video seconds with only mild changes in depth. The vertical hit at 43-46 s lasts about 3 video seconds.
- Between 2.5 s frames the stance often changes from tall to low, so transitions are shorter than 2.5 s; the 38.5-41.7 s crops put the drop from upright to loaded at roughly 1-1.5 video seconds.

## 5. Repeated patterns

- The tube crouch: held low for the whole tube ride, with a slow rise and fall of only about 10-15% hip height. One pattern, one ride; it is the only thing I saw sustained.
- Tall, then back low over the tail, then up again in each turn (seen in two turns in 32-42 s; not enough to call it a guaranteed rule).
- Arms stay at or below shoulder height in every sampled frame until the final vertical hit; they track the trunk rotation.
- The weight stays biased toward the back foot at the loaded part of every turn I could see.

## 6. What I could NOT see

- Left-right lean (camera is side-on, so the rider's tilt toward or away from the camera is only partly visible).
- Foot placement and board rail angle in the tube (the rider is 40-50 px and partly hidden by the wall and foam).
- Anything between my frames, especially the order in which hips, shoulders and board turn.
- Whether any frame in the tube shows the rear knee against the front knee or the front hand touching the wall.
- The real-time speed of the slow motion, so real-time turn durations are unknown.
- 20-28 s: no rider visible in the sampled frames.
- 43.7-47.5 s: the rider is in a vertical/aerial manoeuvre with spray, fine body detail is lost.
- All angles and fractions above are pixel estimates, not measurements.
