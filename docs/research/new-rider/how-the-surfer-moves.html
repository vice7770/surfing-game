Breakline, the new surfer

How the Surfer Moves

On every game tick the controller's input goes into the ride session. The board, the rider and the water are then solved together in small substeps. In each one, the rider's balance chooses two commands, the ankle's rest angle and the upper body's twist torque. It chooses them from what it sensed 0.1 s ago and from its own picture of the board. The water, the fins and those two torques move the board, and a capture-point test decides whether the rider can still stay up.

AttachedRider.ts:5372, :5534; src/physics/humanBalance.ts:472
  • The capture point is where the body's centre of mass is heading: its offset from the feet plus its speed ÷ √(g ÷ height). It is measured along the vertical the rider feels, which is gravity plus the turn's acceleration, in the deck's frame.
  • The balance bar is the least of three margins: the capture point inside the feet, the grip left, and the knees' strength left.
  • The fall. Once the capture point is past the feet's edge by more than the reach, with the weight itself past the edge, fallen (:530) and separate('balance') (AttachedRider.ts:5520).
  • Other ways off. The capture test pauses while the feet carry almost no load (CAPTURE_LOAD). The rider also comes off for a foot slip, a sway or posture error past recovery, or too long in the air (AttachedRider.ts:5519–5522).

The balance loop

The balance never sees the board directly. Its prediction and its steady turn both come from its picture of the board, and they meet in the feedback, 0.1 s behind the senses. When the picture is wrong, its corrections steer the board in ways it does not expect.

Where the turn comes from

  • The stick does not turn the board. It asks for a lean (5e).
  • The balance then rolls the board with the ankle, and twists the upper body, toward the steady turn its picture predicts for that lean (6d–6e).
  • The board turns because its rail and fins meet the water at an angle (4).
  • So the turn needs two things to agree: the balance's picture of how the board will turn, and how the water really turns it.

What goes wrong in Compress and Crouch (measured)

Sample: the bots' rides of this build, Big swell seeds 1–3, the steer from 1.0 to 2.0 s after the stand. Item 2 also counts the test wave; item 3 is round 3.6d's probe of the board.

  1. The balance expects about twice the turn the board makes. Measured in the steer's second half, 1.5–2.0 s after the stand, on 16 Compress rides that stay up, with no switches:
    • the stick asks 38° of lean;
    • the steady turn (6d) expects the board to turn at 45°/s;
    • the board comes round at about 21°/s (10.7° in that half second);
    • the body's bank averages 20° and the rail 17°.

    The model's yaw rate is about the leg's axis and the board's turn here is its heading in the world, so this shows a mismatch in scale, not an exact ratio (Codex's review).

  2. He goes over the inside rail. 51 of the 56 falls during the steer go into the turn, never over the nose or tail. The body leans in, the board swings away under it, and one big swing carries the body over the inside rail.
  3. The picture answers the ankle differently from the board (measured in round 3.6d).
    • After the ankle moves, the board yaws at once: −0.38 rad/s per radian of command within 3 ticks in Compress.
    • The picture says it barely does: +0.007.
    • So the balance's own corrections steer the board in ways it does not expect.
  4. Crouch starts lower.
    • Its bar's lowest point comes before the turn on 14–16 of 36 rides, against 2–6 in Compress.
    • Its knees leave less strength in reserve: a median of 0.50 against 0.60 before the steer.
    • The turn fixes cannot reach that.
  5. The turn comes from the balance's aim (3.6j). With the steady turn's turn-rate and slip targets removed, and the straight-riding fault that exposed fixed, the board came round 16–55° less by +1.5 s on the rides that stayed up, and standing tall turned the wrong way. Without the aim, the water turns the board much less than the aim does.
  6. The water turns the board right for its rail (3.6k). With the rail held in the flat-water lab, the board carves steadily at 30–40° at 7 m/s: about 1.4°/s of turn per degree, and 55°/s at 40°, against Compress's 23°/s today. A real bottom turn sets about 42° of rail in about half a second (Forsyth et al. 2024, as the advisor reads its Table 2). At 6 and 8 m/s a deep rail rings instead of holding, and no sourced rail force fixes that.
  7. The rail comes in slowly, because the body leans in slowly (corrected 2026-11-28: this page said the rail stops at 17–20°, but that was an average over the middle of the steer). In the bots' one-second full-stick turns on Big:
    • By +0.5 s the lean asked is 30–37°, but the body is at 11–12° and the rail at 8–11°.
    • When the steer ends, the body is at 22–30° and the rail at 28° standing tall, 20° in Compress and 19° in Crouch, still rising.
    • Nothing is at a limit. The ankle is at its range on 2–4 % of the steer, and on your address it has no strength limit.
    • The balance's own picture leans just as slowly: 12–16° at +0.5 s and 33–37° at +1.0 s, after first turning the board the wrong way. Standing tall the game nearly matches it; Compress and Crouch fall 9° and 15° behind it by +1.0 s.
    • Held for two seconds, the tall rail runs on to about 37° by +1.5 s and keeps going until he goes over into the turn (9 of 18 fall). In Compress and Crouch 11 of 18 fall, most within the first second.
    • A September study on the old rider found the lean can only come in as fast as the turn's pull builds: a body that leans ahead of its pull falls in.
The coordinator's reading, to be tested: the lean is sized for a turn the board does not make, and each correction swings the board. One big swing carries him over the inside rail.

Where the nudge came from

  • The board aim (turnAim=board) swapped 6d's steady turn for standing tall's, including its commands. 6e then held the board to it.
  • In the steer's second half, the twist torque was about four times larger: 24 N·m against 6.
  • Half a second after the steer, the board had turned 13° against 23° without it (16 Compress rides that stay up). The combination reached 18°.
  • It is withdrawn.
  • Without the switches, the steady turn asks for more turn than the board makes, so on average the balance is not holding the turn back.
