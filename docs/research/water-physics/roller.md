# The roller: whitewater as geometry

The roller is a thin, light lens of aerated water covering the whole front of a broken wave. Built as geometry, as you chose (Q4), it gives broken waves a tumbling white front, and it makes boards bog and get pushed the way real whitewater does.

## Its shape

- **It covers the whole face,** crest to toe. Its length × tan(face angle) ≈ H, so it is about 2.1 H long at onset (25°), 2.5–3.5 H once developed (16–22°), and about 5 H on a steep inner beach ([Martins et al. 2018](https://purehost.bath.ac.uk/ws/files/170060912/Martins_et_al_JGR2018.pdf)).
- **It is a lens on the bore's face.** It is about 0.25–0.5 H thick near the crest and tapers to nothing at the toe, near mean water level (inferred from LiDAR-fitted areas). Its top rises steeply from the toe and flattens toward the crest, like a hydraulic jump.
- **Its life:** short and steep at the break point, it lengthens and eases over about one roller length. It flattens where the beach steepens, and is shed once the bore stops breaking (bore Froude number below about 1.3).

## What it does to a board and rider

These are estimates from proxies. No surf-specific measurement exists.

- **Air:** the roller's fitted mean density is about 0.6–0.87 of water, falling to almost nothing at its top.
- **Bogging:** a prone surfer on a 30 L board cannot float statically once the air fraction passes about 0.28 (0.44 on a 60 L board), and planing lift falls with the mixture's density. The game already lightens water by its air fraction for the hull, so sampling the air in the lens gives this for free. The current air cap of 0.2 is low for the lens itself.
- **Push:** the roller's pull along the face is about 330 × H pascals. That roughly doubles the downhill drive on a prone rider: about 166 N at H = 1 m, against 236 N of gravity along an 18° face.
- **Getting hit:** paddling into a 0.5–1 m roller means about 1.3–2.3 kN for 0.5–0.7 s. This is scaled from tsunami-bore loads ([Yeh et al. 2014](https://icce-ojs-tamu.tdl.org/icce/article/view/7955)).
- **Speed:** whitewater carries a rider no faster than the bore, about 3.4 m/s at H = 0.5 m and 4.9 m/s at 1 m, and the rider falls behind once out of the lens.
- **Duck-diving:** get below the aerated lens, about 0.5–1 m under a 1 m spilling bore. A plunging or shallow break offers no refuge.

## Look

- Surface bumps and fingers that grow from about 0.5 to 3 × the trough depth, pulsing at about 0.1–1 Hz (lab bores scaled up to surf size, which slows them). The toe line wanders, and the droplets stay millimetre-sized.
- Brightest at the crest. Draw the active roller as its own object, separate from the residual foam; the two even look different in infrared.
- No surf game documents its roller as a volume that pushes, lifts or sinks the rider.
