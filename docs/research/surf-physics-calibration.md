# Surf physics calibration for the playable height-field model

This note separates evidence-backed **relationships** from coefficients tuned for play. The current model is a single-layer, CPU height field with four hull contacts, relative-water drag, a lateral rail force, and an art-directed peeling break. It is not a validated simulation of a surfboard on an ocean wave.

## Board support and turning

- A surfboard CFD study varied pitch (0–12°) and nose emergence (0–24 cm), then measured lift, drag, and pitch moment. These forces changed with attitude and wetted length; the estimated center of pressure was about 40% of the chord for moderately emerged cases. This supports calculating support at several contacts and allowing pitch and immersion to affect it, rather than using a single constant lift force. It does **not** calibrate this game's four-contact force law: that study used steady, flat-water flow and an imposed board attitude. [D'Ambrosio, *Hydrodynamic Characterization of Planing Surfboards Using CFD* (2020)](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-3900/49/1/68)
- Pressure sensors on an actual fin during three traverse cycles on a standing river wave measured a pressure difference that varied through the turn. The authors estimated a peak lateral lift from the measured pressure, but explicitly warn that treating the pressure as uniform across the whole fin can overestimate the total force. This supports a **directional** grip force tied to relative sideways water flow and immersion, as `BoardPhysics` does. It does not justify assigning its estimated peak force to an ocean-wave shortboard or this game's `railGrip` constant. [Kniesburges et al., *Measurements of the hydrodynamic pressure on a surfboard fin during surfing* (2025)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-94834-0)
- The implemented force law approximates `F_side ∝ C_L(α) |v_rel|²`, with `α = atan2(v_lateral, max(|v_forward|, ε))`, submersion scaling, and a softened high-angle response. This is a **modeling proposal**, not a coefficient measured by the cited studies. Preserve the rule that yaw input changes board heading while water-relative lateral force changes its path. Compare travel heading and board heading in tests so steering cannot become direct sideways acceleration.
- Keep board support, planing lift, drag, and turning dependent on **velocity relative to local water**, not world speed. The in-water fin measurements combined incident flow with rider travel, and the CFD board study varied incoming speed. The game now uses water-relative tangent speed for planing lift and rail grip, but their coefficients remain gameplay approximations. A later revision can include board pitch and wetted length, then retune against catch and stability tests. [Kniesburges et al. (2025)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-94834-0); [D'Ambrosio (2020)](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-3900/49/1/68)

## Break onset and dissipation

- The US Army Corps' shallow-water equations conserve water volume and depth-averaged horizontal momentum under hydrostatic, depth-uniform-flow assumptions. This is the useful level of analogy for the game's height field. The code sets `g_effective = speed² / meanDepth`, giving the desired deep-water long-wave speed `c ≈ √(g_effective × meanDepth) = speed`. A tunable shelf now decreases local still-water depth, and the elevation update uses local-depth fluxes so the crest slows toward shore while a flat surface stays at rest. This is a qualitative shoaling mechanism, not a calibrated beach wave. Wave gravity remains separate from the board's 9.81 m/s². [USACE HEC-RAS technical reference, *Shallow Water Equations*](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/hecras/beta/technical-reference/hydraulic-equations/shallow-water-equations)
- Laboratory wave-group experiments in intermediate water found breaking onset near `B_x = U_s / C = 0.835 ± 0.005`, where `U_s` is **surface** horizontal water velocity at the crest and `C` is crest-point speed. This is a useful qualitative check: a faster-moving crest relative to its surface water is less likely to break. It is **not** a plug-in threshold here: the game's single-layer flow is depth-averaged, its crest is skewed and scheduled, and its shelf is synthetic rather than measured bathymetry. [Saket et al., *Wave breaking onset of two-dimensional wave groups in uniform intermediate depth water* (2017)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04354)
- A numerical study of depth-induced breaking treated breaking as a separately activated energy sink and checked both energy loss and horizontal momentum conservation. Its breaking zone required onset and termination criteria; the authors compared against laboratory beach and bar cases. This supports activating dissipation locally near the steep crest and tracking whether wave energy falls after breaking. The game's `breakingAt()` uses a timed lateral peel gate plus local slope, now strengthened over the shelf by a bounded depth factor, while `step()` damps horizontal velocity and smooths elevation. That is acceptable for an art-directed spilling break, but it does not establish a physical onset point, conserved momentum, or hydraulic-jump energy budget. [Papoutsellis et al., *Modeling of depth-induced wave breaking in a fully nonlinear free-surface potential flow model* (2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08982)
- Avoid replacing the scheduled peel with a universal `H/h ≈ 0.78` switch. The present field has a synthetic shoaling shelf, but actual depth-limited breaking depends on local wave and seabed conditions beyond this model. The Corps gives 0.78 as a traditional design limit for depth-limited **wave height**, not a spatial peeling rule. [USACE, *Hydrologic Engineering Requirements for Reservoirs*, EM 1110-2-1420 (2018)](https://www.publications.usace.army.mil/Portals/76/Users/182/86/2486/EM_1110-2-1420.pdf)

## Practical calibration checks for this prototype

1. Measure unforced packet propagation and check that crest speed remains near the selected wave speed before breaking. With no board input, check that the field does not gain energy over time.
2. Compare otherwise identical rides with and without breaking. Breaking should reduce coherent crest energy and create a local loss of ride support, without teleporting the board or increasing its speed solely from the damping term. Treat `breakingDissipation` as an internal diagnostic until it is tied to an explicit physical energy integral.
3. For turning, compare matched left/right input, zero input, and reduced immersion. Lateral path change should reverse with steering and weaken with little water contact. Tune feel against the supplied gameplay video, not against fin force numbers from a different board and standing river wave.
4. Keep coefficients such as `0.035` planing lift, `0.18` riding fin force, `0.45` break damping, and the 2.8 m/s peel speed documented as **game constants**. No cited experiment determines them for this geometry, water field, rider, or camera scale.

## Claims to avoid

Do not call the current breaker a Navier–Stokes or Boussinesq simulation, a resolved plunging barrel, or a validated shoaling model. Do not infer actual fin lift, board pressure distribution, or surfing performance from its diagnostic values. The cited experiments and models establish plausible force directions and wave-energy behavior; quantitative validation would require a defined board and fin geometry, measured water-relative velocities, surveyed bathymetry, and comparison cases.
