# Exact demanded contact rows: source feasibility

Examined repository `/Users/regina/Desktop/Projects/surfing-game`, HEAD `58ceb6a29617c00f93fb3b21eff3058d824319d5`. Source-only audit: no production edits, tests, build, benchmark, or browser run. This note does not claim a measured gain or geometry acceptance.

## Conclusion

An exact demanded-contact path is feasible, but a clipped-front `SweptLoft.build` is not exact. The lowest-risk boundary is **global original slice/profile/XZ/topology preparation, followed by demanded vertex Y and demanded normals**, rather than trying to make all front planning local. This removes actual global water-height interpolation and normal construction, while retaining the expensive dependencies that define the original geometry. It is a new candidate; its savings and demanded-row fraction have not been measured. The previous whole-contact skip failure and crest-ray-plan microcost result are accepted and were not repeated.

## Why all planning cannot simply be clipped

`src/wave/barrel/sweptLoft.ts:319–344` surveys every front for the shared live/refinement budget. This chooses spacing and the budgeted-clock path before any geometry. `planRays` (line 482) prepares one complete-front `CrestRayPlan`; its stabilization blend depends on the complete captured controls, scale maxima, tangent progress, shifted knots and nearby derivative pairs (`crestRays.ts:117–225`). Local subsets would change the rays. Budgeted clocks also depend on the preceding live planned clock (`sweptLoft.ts:495–506`). Keep all of this and the original record/front/sigma order.

Overlaps are decided globally from the **drawn** lifted footprint even in contact mode (`sweptLoft.ts:970–1043`), and resting strips can retire an earlier strip. Thus later-front preparation can change an earlier front's final run. `sealRuns` (line 867) finds the final surviving run endpoints and tapers their vertices onto water over `endBlend`. A local strip cannot choose its own endpoints. Keep exact footprint/rest computation, original ordered overlap loop, final `sliceJoined`, run endpoints, and seal weights globally. `forwardRest` (line 799) reads solver water along each slice to choose its drawn reach; these reads are necessary for exact overlap decisions and are not deferred by the first candidate.

## Concrete minimal split

1. Add an explicit contact preparation path while leaving existing public `SweptLoft.build` and `SweptContact.update` eager. Perform the original survey, refinements, complete-front ray plans, contact `profileAt`, all slice metadata, original Float32 XZ stores, drawn landmarks, forward rest, footprint overlap decisions and final run seal metadata. Keep slice IDs and vertex numbering unchanged. Profile/still-level/height recipes must be captured per row instead of depending on shared mutable scratch arrays.
2. Preserve the existing cell index and captured projections using those exact Float32 XZ coordinates. The cell counting sort already preserves increasing strip order (`sweptContact.ts:457–514`). The first-front answer and half-open ray tests, exact nanometre nudge, triangle order, lower-index edge tie rule and stable insertion sort must remain unchanged. Buckets are already demanded, so merely moving their creation is not new work removal.
3. On a ray-bracketed candidate strip, materialize the original Y values for its two rows before crossings. `floorAt` does not need normals. A successful `query` needs the three chosen vertices' normal values. For full-row normal preparation, strip `s` needs row positions `s−1` through `s+2`, clamped to that **final surviving run**. Across-profile neighbors are `j±1`, also clamped. `normals` (line 1080) has no farther dependency. Cache each row and normal row once per step. A finer vertex-normal path is possible later, but complete-row preparation is the simpler first implementation.
4. `heldByAnother` (sweptContact line 304) tests XZ triangles only. It can retain ordered candidates without constructing their Y or normals. `sliceLife`, tangent, lip material velocity and lip weight are already slice metadata; `query` interpolates only the two rows' values (lines 243–261), so they do not force remote row Y preparation.
5. Contact queries never read `indices`, mask, lift, sheet or throat. In ordinary contact mode, sheet measurement is off and formed/throat contribution is zero (`sweptLoft.ts:276,639`). Therefore no throat, mouth, or triangle-index output is needed for normal runtime queries. Keep the original eager API and an explicit full-materialization inspection fallback to satisfy existing full-loft tests/probes and public `last` behavior. Do not accidentally access a full-materializing `last` getter inside the runtime query path.

## Numerical traps: preserve the original stages

`ProfileLibrary.pointAt` is not a bit-exact substitute for selecting a coordinate from `profileAt`: `profileAt` stores each case interpolation into Float32, stores the blended Float32 result, then stores its scaled Float32 result (`ProfileLibrary.ts:207–220,369–382`). `pointAt` carries those stages in Float64 (`ProfileLibrary.ts:236–248,355–367`). Keep the original whole Float32 profile in the first candidate; a sparse exact profile sampler would need to reproduce all three stores and requires separate parity proof.

The first vertex `heightAt(px,pz)` sees **double** coordinates from original anchor, ray and profile arithmetic before the Float32 position stores (`sweptLoft.ts:744–757`). A later seal read sees the already-rounded Float32 XZ (`sweptLoft.ts:891–896`). Keep the original per-row Float32 profile and **double** ray/anchor/initial weight/rest recipe, or equivalently capture exact double first-read coordinates and weights. Stored `sliceRayX`, `sliceWeight` and `lift` alone lose needed precision. Reproduce the first Y Float32 assignment and the separate seal Y Float32 assignment; a single final-weight formula is not exact. Likewise seal endpoint distance uses stored Float32 sigma and its branch uses stored Float32 slice weight. Keep those stages, not an unrounded metadata shortcut.

Projections are captured at update and lazy buckets deliberately keep using them even if public loft positions/topology later change (`sweptContact.test.ts:372`). If `last` becomes a getter, materialize all output before exposing it and retain captured index/projection data. Existing callers can mutate the returned arrays; the current query uses mutated triangles with the original captured index and projections. Do not silently change this contract by rebuilding the index from exposed data.

## Height epoch and cache: real implementation requirement

The generic `heightAt` callback has no purity or stability guarantee. Moving its calls after `update` changes behavior for callbacks with mutable state or side effects. Preserve eager public APIs; make demand construction an explicit physical-runner mode with a stable height epoch.

Ordinary `SurfZoneRunner.updateContact` currently wraps only the eager update in `withSurfaceNodeCache` (`SurfZoneRunner.ts:488–499`). That cache is invalidated at both scope edges (`PhysicalSurfWater.ts:132–146`). Deferred reads after the scope cannot retain its caching advantage unless the scope or explicit epoch extends through the ordinary body/gauge demand. Physical body reactions only mutate `qx/qz` (`PhysicalSurfWater.ts:284–297`); plain height uses h/bed, fixed rest level, render grid and no carve at swept spots. This supports a narrowly owned post-water/pre-next-water height epoch, but import/reset/window/grid changes and nonordinary callers must end it or force eager fallback. Do not let a lazy recipe survive to another water step or window. Extending a cache over flow queries is harmless only because it caches heights, not flow.

## Lowest-risk memo ownership

The smallest code change for ordinary synchronous demand is to widen the existing `PhysicalSurfWater.withSurfaceNodeCache` scope to include preparation **and the entire synchronous `afterWater` body/gauge work**, with no nested update-only scope that invalidates between them. That directly reuses the proven integer render-node cache instead of inventing a contact-level Map keyed by floating query coordinates. `afterWater` contains no await, and board reactions change only q. A per-query `withSurfaceNodeCache(() => ensureRows(...))` is also exact within the stable epoch but discards reuse from global forward-rest preparation and earlier demanded rows at every query; it could erase much of the expected saving. A memo of whole `heightAt(x,z)` results would also miss reuse among distinct vertices sharing the same 4×4 render-node stencil.

If lazy construction must remain available outside that synchronous phase, a narrowly owned **height-node epoch** in PhysicalSurfWater is preferable to a private contact interpolation cache: start after water completes, capture h/bed/grid identity and window addressing, retain the same integer node stamps across forward-rest and row construction, end before the next water step/import/window/grid change. Public `runner.water` can be sampled by nonordinary clients, and `fill` has a gauge fallback when `wave` is absent (`SurfZoneRunner.ts:780`); do not claim the narrow afterWater scope covers every caller. A physical mode must explicitly define how outside-phase reads preserve the previous contact height: either support the full stable epoch through those reads, constrain/guard the mode to ordinary initialized gameplay, or retain the eager path. It cannot use a new solver height with an old contact recipe after the epoch expires. Existing generic update/build remains the reference and nonordinary fallback.

The cache must contain height nodes only; do not retain flow/velocity answers because q changes during board reactions. Timing attribution must charge demanded work to contact even when it executes under board/gauge calls; simply reducing `contactMs` while increasing `boardMs` is not a gain.

## Work actually removed, and what remains unknown

At the 40,000-vertex loft budget, eager Y preparation can interpolate water at up to one vertex per sample, seal can read again at cut ends, and normals evaluate central differences and square roots across every joined row. Demanding a subset of rows removes those operations for all other rows. It can also avoid runtime-only unused triangle-index writes. Whole-profile interpolation, all XZ, all metadata, forward rests, global overlap decisions and the cell index remain eager in the first boundary. This deliberately does not claim to remove the entire measured contact cost.

The 185 gauge queries and 54 near-front points do not reveal the number of distinct demanded strips or their halo union. A source-only audit cannot infer the saving from query count. Before committing a prototype, capture distinct strip/row demands against the original eager contact in one ordinary late interval, including misses and later-front XZ candidates. Then verify full ContactHit/floor results and complete forced-materialization bytes against the eager reference across refinement, budget clamps, fades, overlap cuts, run edges, held contact, repeated queries, empty fronts, reused builders and window/height-epoch changes. Only after parity should paired ordinary gameplay timing justify adoption.
