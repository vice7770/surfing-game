# Shorter Padang physical domain: source-only feasibility

## Conclusion

A materially shorter physical domain would remove actual solver, field, readback, and snapshot work; it is a larger architectural candidate than the already-rejected resolution/FFT changes. It is **not** a drop-in change to `tank.offshore`, and a plain deep-water sinusoidal target at −400 or −300 m cannot be claimed to retain the present nearshore crest/tube timing. Both cuts lie on the real ramp, far shoreward of the 25 m forcing platform; −400 m already cuts into the focus spur and −300 m removes required crest sizing history at the peak. The source contains useful WKB phase/amplitude transforms, but those are linear initialization/side-feed models, not evidence that they recreate the evolved original 2D nonlinear incoming field.

The lowest sensible design boundary is a new, explicitly versioned nearshore-domain policy that keeps the original global sea/clock and original physical bathymetry/nearshore resolution, supplies incoming local-depth phase/flux and reflected-wave handling consistently on CPU and GPU, and preserves crest prehistory needed by the library. It must pass matched wave/crest/tube and gameplay gates before adoption. No code, tests, build, benchmark, GPU/browser operation, Git operation, source edit, or configuration change was performed for this note. Counts below are source arithmetic and retained-fixture metadata, not measured FPS or a quality verdict.

## Where the domain and sea are chosen

- `src/game/SurfConditions.ts:89–92`: Big Padang is buoy Hs 3.8 m, Tp 18 s, direction 0°, spreading 150. `PhysicalMode.ts:502` gives ordinary Padang `dx:2, fineSpacing:1` only when no supplied room/replay/report overrides exist; the GPU tier supplies 64 components. The retained ordinary Big fixture uses seed 8761, tide 0, calm wind, stage 2, dx 2, 64 components.
- `src/wave/SurfZoneSimulation.ts:212–244`, `tankLayout`: Padang uses `PADANG.deep=25` m. It scans the spot bed across the entire 320 m alongshore window, every 5 m, for the deepest first set-break location; sets use `min(0.9*(25+tide), 1.27*komarGaughan(Hs,Tp)/0.78)`. Fine rows start 40 m seaward of that first break. The 80 m blend must lie beyond the forereef foot. The offshore relaxation zone is `max(60 m, 0.75*waveKinematics(Tp,25).wavelength)`. `SurfZoneConfig` currently exposes dx/fine/coarse spacing, spin-up periods, lead, and sea time, but no offshore-domain override.
- `Bathymetry.ts:101–145`: the current bed is 25 m deep beyond its 1:20 forereef; a 1:80 ramp rises from 12 m knee to the wedge's 7 m base. The wedge has a 1:15 slope across the angled crest (library 1:19 along the wave path), 1.25 m flat, peak x −60/z −170, and focus relief 2 m over 150 m seaward of the wedge base. Channel and beach geometry remain separate parts of the actual bed.
- Current arithmetic gives wedge base z −282.591378704909, knee −682.591378704909, forereef foot −942.591378704909, zoneInner −1032.591378704909, blendEnd −952.591378704909. At Big mid tide the set-depth estimate is 9.269224611694 m; the deepest first set break is z −465 m on the upcoast bare ramp, so fineFrom is −505 m. `stretchedEdges` produces 190 stretched offshore rows plus 535 one-metre rows to shore +30: 725 rows ×160 columns =116,000 cells. The retained fixture's offshore/render zMin is −1233.0249111256337 m, yielding a ~200.433532 m relaxation zone and ~1263.024911 m physical extent.
- `SurfZoneSimulation.ts:563–566` builds `stretchedEdges(offshore,shore,fineFrom,1,4)` and initializes `tankDepth`. `tankDepth` (`:462–466`) blends constant 25 m depth onto the actual spot only over zoneInner→blendEnd. Thus choosing a new zoneInner near the shore while retaining this bed recipe would flatten/change part of the real ramp and focus; it is not merely eliminating empty offshore cells.

Changing only offshore to −400/−300 while leaving the current fineFrom −505 is structurally invalid: in `ShallowWaterSolver.ts:59–82`, the coarse span becomes negative and the next fine edge moves back behind the first edge. The ordered layout contract `offshore < zoneInner < blendEnd < fineFrom < shore` and the zone-length/linear-generation tests would fail. A shorter model needs its own valid grid/layout/forcing separation; it must not satisfy these old guards by silently changing the bed, shrinking the fine zone, or relaxing their intended wave contract.

## Cell and render work that can actually disappear

These are **geometric lower bounds before new coupling/absorber buffers or prehistory support**. Both candidate cuts are inside the original 1 m physical zone. Retaining existing nearshore cell locations and dropping original cell centres seaward of an integer cut gives:

| Physical seaward edge | Remaining rows ×160 | Remaining cells | Removed rows / cells | Cell reduction | 2 m render nodes at unchanged 320 m width |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Original −1233.024911 | 725×160 | 116,000 | 0 /0 | 0% | 161×633=101,913 |
| −400 m | 430×160 | 68,800 | 295 /47,200 | 40.69% | 161×216=34,776 |
| −300 m | 330×160 | 52,800 | 395 /63,200 | 54.48% | 161×166=26,726 |

Each complete surface/flow/aeration snapshot has six Float32 values per render node, so nominal payloads would fall from 2,445,912 bytes to 834,624 /641,424 bytes for those hypothetical node grids. Bed initialization, mapping, physical sampling cache, geometry, and surface/flow/aeration textures also shrink. These grid counts deliberately use the requested cut as the new origin; see the interpolation-origin caveat below before interpreting them as a parity design.

The current mask has independent 1 m resolution (`WaterSurface.createMaskGrid`, :635–644) and follows the rounded physical render rectangle: baseline 321×1265=406,065 mask nodes. New exact −400/−300 origins give 321×431=138,351 /321×331=106,251 nodes. Barrel loft/profile sampling density and local geometry must remain unchanged; shrinking the water rectangle alone is not permission to simplify the tube or clip the library.

Global work removed spans Boussinesq stencil/predictor/rate/viscosity/implicit column operations and CPU/GPU h/q/strength/rate/predictor packing/readback, plus per-cell Breaking, FoamField/AerationField/advection storage/loops, crash whitewater arrays, front's column-crest scan, and snapshot interpolation. Some source lists, particles, front sorting, loft construction, and rendering remain driven by the same nearshore break. CFL substep count need not shrink merely because row count does: the retained smallest cells/viscosity may still control it. No proportional timing/FPS claim follows from the counts.

## Forcing, spin-up, and stage-2 blending contracts

`surfZoneSea` (`SurfZoneSimulation.ts:365–380`) creates the deterministic original component set at 25 m+tide, with deep-water Hs shoaled to the edge (`edgeHeight`). Padang always uses the stage-2 Madsen–Sørensen wave number, not an arbitrary shallow-water/renderer wave number. Components retain omega, random phase, direction, amplitude, kx, and kz (`SeaState.ts:100–140`). Rebuilding the SeaState at 8 m/5 m with the same seed is not the same incoming sea: dispersion changes kx/kz, and local phase and group arrival change even if random phases repeat.

At construction (`SurfZoneSimulation.ts:573–585`), spin-up is default 2*Tp=36 simulated seconds. `planSetRun` chooses the next seeded set-envelope peak at x=0, **old zoneInner**, after lead+spin-up (default lead 25 s); `seaTimeOffset` is either this warm-start sea time or `startSeaTime-spinUp`. `warmStart` fills the whole original tank with a column WKB transform from zoneInner. Spin-up advances real CPU/GPU water at freshly checked CFL limits, then starts breaking history. A new referenceZ fed into `planSetRun` can select a different group peak or its timing; retaining the old seed alone does not retain the planned wave. Shorter propagation also changes settling time, generated harmonics, reflected-wave history, and the dispersive predictor state. Do not disguise that shift with an arbitrary global clock offset.

`SeaStateBoundary` is a constant-depth linear target. Its per-cell eta is Σ A cos(kx*x+kz*z+phase−omega*(solverTime+offset)); flux is Σ(omega/k)*direction*eta_component. It precomputes phase at each weighted row, and re-factors x after window shifts. `zoneWeightsAlongZ` uses the Jacobsen ramp from weight 0 at inner to 1 at outer. CPU `ShallowWaterSolver.relax` blends h toward `max(0,target.eta-bed)` and q toward prescribed flux **after each solver substep** (tide/current target convention must remain identical to current source, not be casually repaired during this experiment).

On GPU, `GpuBoussinesq.ts:46–66` only accepts one `SeaStateBoundary` followed by optional `SideFeed`; a new arbitrary callback zone would reject the device and run on CPU. `packComponents` (:122–140) folds original global x/time phase in Float64 then uploads Float32 amplitude/kx/kz/speed/phase/omega. `boussinesqWgsl.ts:617–640` implements the same constant-component relaxation, after the substep and before side feed. Supporting a local-depth, spatially varying boundary requires a deliberate device data/kernel contract, not changing just CPU target logic or pretending it is the existing constant-depth class.

Cross-shore ends currently reflect: ShallowWaterSolver's z flux mirrors normal velocity at both ends (:720 onward); Boussinesq z derivatives and column recovery mirror Q as odd (:743 onward, :859 onward). The current ~0.75-wavelength relaxation zone damps those end reflections before they reach the game. A much shorter buffer needs a defensible incoming/outgoing separation or absorber on **all** relevant stage-2 stencils/implicit recovery paths. An incoming sinusoid imposed at a nearby reflective wall is not the current absorption contract. The stage-2 column solve spans the retained domain; even unchanged local grid/h does not promise bit identity when its boundary/system size changes.

## Explicit phase/depth coupling that a candidate would need

A plausible linear seam must keep the **original** SeaState and global seaTimeOffset/room clock/plan reference. From the old reference line to each new coupling point, preserve each component's omega and kx, compute local `k(omega,stillDepth)` with the **same solver** dispersion, use `kz=sqrt(k²−kx²)` while alive, integrate local kz across the original bed, and scale amplitude by conserved cross-shore group flux. Flux direction/speed follows local kz/k, not the 25 m direction/speed. Tide and x-dependent depth must enter the same transform, with explicit nonpropagating/dry/capped cases. This needs a spatial phase/amplitude/flux table, not a single reused deep-water sin/cos coefficient.

`warmStart.transformedSea` already expresses those rules, with trapezoid phase accumulation, depth cap gamma=0.78, and per-column reuse; `SideFeed.build` derives its own row factors from it. They are a useful prototype boundary for an unproved linear candidate, **not exact replacement authority**. Their straight-contours-per-column assumption omits 2D propagation/refraction/diffraction/interference across the focus and reef. The old nonlinear Boussinesq solution carries bound/free harmonics, current, reflected energy, eddy-viscosity state, and predictor memory absent from a first-order linear target. The existing Padang generation test (`SurfZoneSimulation.test.ts:197–211`) explicitly requires Schäffer's first-order-generation measure S≤1.2 at the 25 m platform; its comments retain the earlier 10 m injection shape drift. A new shallow seam must confront that numerical/physical contract instead of treating the current FarFieldOcean or WKB warm start as proof of quality.

## Actual dependencies and nearshore escape paths

1. **Crest/library timing and prehistory.** `BARREL_SPOTS.padang` fixes h0=7 m, slope 1:19, and measured join/throw tables; generated Basilisk cases (`scripts/barrel-library.ts`) are external local transect data, not tied to the global tank length. `sliceClock.ts:16–70` sizes throw depth by crest at the foot and join by its maximum over the 6–5 m band. `barrelFrontFrom` defaults to **zoneInner**, and `columnCrests` scans every crest from there in ordered columns/rows before physical tracking. `BreakingFront.ts:334` only assigns initial footHeight if still depth≥h0−1 m; an unsized track cannot join. A shorter domain must retain actual original foot crossing and band history, not assign proxy heights to late crests or weaken the join gate. The focus spur starts at z −432.591379 m. At x=−60 and mid tide, original actual depth at −400 is about 8.243548 m; at −300 it is 5.283343 m. Thus −400 removes the first ~32.6 m of focus propagation; −300 enters below the 6 m first-seen sizing floor at the peak and removes already-needed foot history. Nearby x/tides require the full bed, not this single transect example.
2. **Side feed.** Padang's 30 m side strips (`SideFeed`) prescribe the transformed sea from original zoneInner, stop at gamma*depth/steep-slope thresholds, and fade over 40 m. `afterShift` rebuilds those factors. Moving reference/boundary changes phase, amplitude, ending row, and feed forcing. Shrinking alongshore was already handled separately; do not repeat that experiment or silently omit side feed.
3. **Rider/board/spawn/resets.** Take-off is scanned from zoneInner using the original bed and edge-height/index (`takeOffPoint`, :417–443). Runner normal rider lineup is focus.z−6 m; board-only lineup is focus.z−25 m. Explicit `spawnOut`, retry `spawnAt`, and `place` accept user/lesson/replay placement. PhysicalSurfWater marks samples outside the actual first/last cell edges as outsideDomain; Runner rescues/resets the board/surfer there (:531–545). A new offshore edge changes where a player can paddle or where a replay placement is valid. Check all supported play, lesson, online and explicit spawn extents; do not infer “nearshore game” means these APIs disappear. Alongshore shifts move water/bed/zone weights and must rebuild any seam tables; they do not supply cross-shore prehistory.
4. **Gauge/contact.** WaveFrameGauge uses a 0.5 m line reaching 40 m behind and up to 52 m ahead of the rider (185 points), plus along-crest curl samples out to 40 m (`physics/waveFrame.ts`). Near-edge samples would see flat outside sea or truncated contact. Keep a declared support margin around supported spawns/rides, and include body/submerged samples, direct floorAt and barrel normal halos. Shortening global water does not authorize geometry substitution.
5. **Render and physical interpolation origin.** `renderGrid` uses tank.offshore as zMin, rounds extent/spacing, and feeds the worker's immutable init grid, bed, buffer sizes and all fields. PhysicalSurfWater Catmull-Rom nodes use the solver's actual first edge as zMin and actual spacing. Baseline −1233.024911 is not congruent to −400/−300 on a 2 m grid; a naive new origin re-phases every nearshore render/physical node and changes interpolation even with identical nearshore h/bed. A quality candidate must explicitly preserve the old sampling anchor or validate the resampling change; “copied heights” alone is insufficient. The 1 m barrel mask uses its own extent/origin; masks, bathymetry textures, mapping and contact height epochs must update consistently.
6. **Far field is a visual continuation.** `PhysicalMode.ts:556–597` rebuilds tankLayout and surfZoneSea, constructs the far-field hole at physical offshore→shore, and anchors FarFieldProfile at **tank.offshore**. Beyond it the bed is artificial deepening toward FAR_DEPTH; beside it the profile follows the edge-column bed. `FarFieldProfile` integrates WKB outward, using Airy dispersion offshore and solver dispersion shoreward; it stores Float32 phase/amplitude and shader sharpening. It does not provide physical q, evolved crest history, breaking or reflections. Moving the hole/reference without a shared seam changes its phase and manufactured seabed, and can create a visible water/normal/chop transition. The worker's new domain/anchor must be explicit init metadata consumed by the main thread, not independently guessed from an old config layout.
7. **Exports/rooms/replays/device layout.** `SurfZoneSimulation.importState` rejects nx/nz or array lengths from another tank. SET1 serialization contains dimensions, clock/offset, fields, lip and front histories; its state does not migrate between domains. A global unversioned tankLayout change for the same room config breaks existing handovers and saved lessons/replays. It also changes Boussinesq layout constants/still slopes/gaps, side tables and source indices. Any new policy needs explicit config/version compatibility and old-domain preservation for supplied recorded states; no truncation of historical state arrays or imported front row metadata is an authorized migration.

## Gates needed before any adoption

Use the original long tank as reference under identical seeds/conditions, the **same global sea time**, and supported rider inputs. Fix policy/tolerances before inspecting the result; no new weak visual acceptance is implied here. Existing `scripts/drift-report.ts` supplies an authoritative onset gate: every matched take-off wave within 0.2 s and 1 m (three columns at focus x and ±20 m), with no missing/extra waves. That is a useful minimum, not full tube/crest quality authority.

Also retain the existing Padang linear-generation/geometry/onset contracts, and compare per-wave crest phase and height across foot and 6–5 m band, face/trough shape and steepest rise, local q/crest speed/angle, breaking depth/time, nearshore h/bed/flow/rate/predictor state, reflection/spin-up transients, and long-run set identity. `padangPhase`, `padangCrest`, front/peel/contact/loft/crash probes already identify the needed observables; their old seeds or weaker scalar Hs alone do not certify the ordinary Big sea. Track actual footHeight/refHeight, sized/unsized/lost/jump counts, front/throw joins, tau/pacing/exit/jet/touchdown and loft geometry/contact support, including all tides and seeds. Test supported spawn/paddle/rescue/retry/restore/room late-join ranges, actual gauge traces, and matched far-field seam geometry/phase/normal/chop views. Finally measure the complete original-vs-candidate path including new seam/absorber/table work, field capture/copy/readback/snapshot, and actual browser/GPU rendering; cell-count savings alone are not a performance result.

The earlier dx=4 whole-grid and FFT=30 candidates are not re-evaluated here. This note proposes no implementation and no proven shortened quality tier. The concrete blockers are the constant-depth device boundary, nearby reflective stage-2 endpoint, nonlinear/2D incoming field, crest foot prehistory (especially −300), interpolation anchoring, and versioned state compatibility.
