# Rejected offline catch-up default

The final offline default is restored to `MAX_BATCH_STEPS=1`, publishing every fixed physics step. The experimental two-step default was rejected after its passive ordinary-game sample. Explicit host batching remains supported, including `maxBatchSteps=2`; the six-step pending queue cap and online room-clock policy with its existing larger queue/batch inference are unchanged. Frozen preview 4202 retains the rejected experiment as evidence; final preview 4201 retains the one-step tube build.

The 90-second experiment retained 60 rendered frames/s and increased observed physics progress to 56.8 fixed steps/s (`0.947×` wall time), compared with 53.2 steps/s (`0.887×`) in the earlier single-step sample. Fresh-water publication rate fell from 53.2 to 28.64/s, with a 33.8 ms median interval; 98.4% of clock advances carried two steps. The small simulation-rate gain did not justify nearly halving water cadence. These were separate samples, so the difference is descriptive rather than a precise causal estimate. Raw metrics, configuration and immutable artifact fingerprints are in [the catch-up report](ordinary-padang-big-fps-catchup-batch2.md) and [its raw JSON](ordinary-padang-big-fps-catchup-batch2.json).

The scheduling reason remains valid: sharing snapshot filling, status calculation and transfer work can increase simulation capacity under backlog. It cannot reduce physics work per step or recover time already discarded above the queue cap. Two-step requests still execute sequential unchanged `1/60` second steps, but expose only their final state; physics rate, publication rate and display FPS must remain separate measurements.

After restoring the default, `npx vitest run src/game/WorkerSurfZone.test.ts` passed 23 tests in 4.49 seconds. Existing default queue/count expectations again require one step per publication. The controlled catch-up tests now explicitly request `maxBatchSteps=2`: they prove one advance in flight, `1,2,1` draining, exact queue overflow accounting, latest held controls, and single-use presses, placement and reactions. Explicit batching overrides retain their behavior. CPU and asynchronous CPU-device stand-ins retain exact final exported physical state, complete active visual fields, both steps' landing events and first-step-only presses compared with two single advances; snapshot knock retains the two-step maximum. Strict typechecking passed during the experiment. These tests establish the supported host-batch contract, not real WebGPU timing. No solver, time step, conservation law or event filtering changed.
