# Rideability report · stage 2 (Boussinesq) solver

Generated by `npm run report:rideability -- ` on 2026-09-29 (plan §4 item 11; reported, not asserted).

**Conditions.** The Wave Lab defaults: Hs 1.4 m, Tp 10 s, 10° from shore-normal, spreading s 12, tide 0 m, calm wind. Game resolution: 1 m surf-zone cells and a 160 m along-shore window. Each spot runs seeds 1–3 for 20 peak periods after the spin-up. The peel is sampled once per period from the onsets of that period.

**Reading it.** A wave is makeable by a skill level when its peel angle reaches that level's minimum (Hutt, Black & Mead 2001: professional 27°, advanced 29°, intermediate 40°, beginner 60°). The shares are cumulative: every beginner wave is also a professional wave. A close-out peels at under 27°. "Mixed" means the onset fit found several peaks at once, so no single angle applies. **Known limits.**
- Ride-time distributions need a rider in the physical mode (P4), so they are not reported yet.
- The stage 2 solver is Madsen–Sørensen Boussinesq with Kennedy eddy-viscosity breaking (P5), its breaking age carried from behind each front face. Inherited from any neighbour, breaking ran along crests as a fuse and roughly doubled every spot's peel speed (docs/research/breaking-age.md). Its dispersion keeps shoaling waves from breaking early. Where contours meet the swell nearly square (the Beach, Point and Reef), the break has less along-crest gradient to peel along and closes out more often than on a shaped bed.
- The peel angle takes √(g h_b) for the crest's speed at breaking. Surf-zone crests run about 1.2–1.27 times faster (Tissier et al. 2013), so the angles read about 5–7° low against the skill ladder's angles, which were measured on photos. That is a separate change.
- The peel is measured over the along-shore window only, with 10 % of the columns at each open edge left out.
- The peel is measured along the fitted break line (onset time and cross-shore position against x), so an oblique break line, such as the Reef's ledge, reads its real speed. The peel speed is the break point's speed along that line: the speed a rider must hold.
