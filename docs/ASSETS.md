# Third-party assets

Every image, model, texture and sound the game ships, with its source and licence. Nothing here is known to need in-game attribution: all of it is CC0 except the generated sounds, which are used under the owner's ElevenLabs plan (see Sounds). The one ported piece of code, under Code, is zlib-licensed, which asks for no in-game credit either. An in-game credits screen becomes necessary only if a CC-BY asset comes in, or if the ElevenLabs plan's terms ask for credit.

## Skies (G7)

The download script moves each sun out of its HDR into a measured directional light (`scripts/assets/skyMath.ts`), so the environment lights the shade and the light casts the shadows without counting the sun twice. The visible sky is Poly Haven's tonemapped JPEG, resized to 4096 × 2048.

## Surfers (G7)

Four bodies built from MakeHuman's base mesh with [MPFB 2](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/mpfb.html) in headless Blender (`scripts/assets/build_surfers.py`, recipes in `scripts/assets/surfers.json`). They share MPFB's Mixamo-compatible skeleton (52 bones), so Mixamo clips can be retargeted onto them later. Each GLB holds:
- the full body (`LOD0`, about 14.5k vertices);
- a low-poly proxy body (`LOD1`, about 1.9k);
- eyes, eyebrows, eyelashes and hair.

Geometry is meshopt-compressed. Textures are WebP at most 2048 px.

The skin, eye, eyebrow, eyelash, hair and proxy assets come from the [MakeHuman system asset pack](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/assets/assetpacks/makehuman_system_assets.html), which lists every item as CC0 by the MakeHuman team. [MakeHuman's licence FAQ](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/makehuman/faq/can_i_sell_models_created_with_makehuman.html) states that exported models are CC0; MPFB itself is GPL tooling and does not ship in the game.

## Sounds (S1)

Fourteen recordings, two candidates for each of seven sounds (roar, distant surf, bubbles, the two lip crashes, the paddle splash and the plunge), **generated with ElevenLabs Sound Effects on 2026-10-03**. The owner chose generated recordings over CC0 downloads that day. They are not CC0: they are used under the owner's ElevenLabs plan, and `public/assets/audio/sounds.json` records them with the licence `generated`. The plan's tier and terms are not written down here: confirm they cover a shipped game, and whether they ask for credit, before release. (The only hint of the tier is that the generator served 44.1 kHz PCM, which the tool's documentation reserves for the Pro tier and above.)

Wind, rush, rail, the pop-up, the click, the chime, the leash snap, the knock and the duck-dive stay synthesised. Every sound keeps its synthesised fallback (`src/audio/synth.ts`), used until its recording loads or if it fails.

Every file is AAC (`.m4a`, 44.1 kHz), 3.3 MB for the fourteen: mono at a 128 kb/s target, except the two `distant` loops (stereo at 256 kb/s, the same 128 kb/s per channel) and the three one-shots the fourth pass repaired (mono at 192 kb/s; see AAC, below). The manifest picks one candidate per sound and sets a gain, and a candidate may carry a `level` of its own (below). All three are **provisional**: they come from measurements and spectrograms, not from listening, and the sound check (the Sound check button in the Wave Lab's settings panel, shown with dev tools on) and the listening playtest are where they get settled. A gain matches the picked recording's loudness to the synthesised sound it replaces: integrated loudness for a loop, the loudest 400 ms for a one-shot.

**Second pass.** The first set was reviewed the same day and rebuilt where it failed. The review found the roar and distant loops Gaussian at the fine scale, statistically like the synthesised noise they replace (detrended kurtosis 2.9–3.3), with `roar-1`'s spectrum nearly flat and `distant-2`'s close to `roar-1`'s; the generator's lines left in the quiet takes; a loop marker at a join and a single-pitched 2 kHz whistle in the underwater loops; and clipped source takes and stray clicks in some one-shots. The six loops and four one-shots were rebuilt from 119 new takes (37 used); `lipJet-2`, `lipRoller-1`, `lipRoller-2` and `paddle-1` are the first pass's files, unchanged.

**Third pass.** A second review of the rebuilt set found:
- the roars still Gaussian below 4 kHz (1–4 kHz kurtosis 3.04 and 3.26, 10 ms micro-variation 1.13 and 1.17 dB, where white noise reads about 0.8), with the picked roar's 63 Hz octave 22 dB under its 500 Hz octave;
- every loop a ring of four 4.5 s chunks, because the tool stops at 5 s, so the surge pattern repeats every 16 s;
- a 3.4 kHz whistle and a brick-wall low-pass in `bubbles-1`, a 7 dB hole, pings and a brick-wall low-pass in `bubbles-2`, and the two `distant` loops almost the same sound, mono, played centred;
- in the one-shots, a ring and a tick in `lipJet-1`, bodies that arrive 0.3–0.4 s late in `lipJet-2`, `lipRoller-1` and `lipRoller-2`, bloops in both paddles, a sub-kick in `plunge-1`, and truncated heads in `paddle-2` and `plunge-2`;
- one file per sound, replayed as often as every 0.18 s (lip jets), 0.25 s (rollers) or 0.45 s (strokes), and the two candidates of a one-shot 2.2–3.8 dB apart in loudness.

It asked for single 30 s takes made through the API, pools of short one-shots played in turn, the bubbles kept out of the underwater muffle, and a treble roll-off with distance. **This pass did the two roars and the code, and nothing else.** After 132 takes (33,289 credits by the API's own per-request cost) the API refused the next 34 requests with `quota_exceeded` (a quota of 90,000 credits, none left), so the other twelve files stayed the second pass's. A fourth pass the same day remade seven of them from takes in hand (below); the rest is outstanding (see Known limits).

**Fourth pass.** The quota stayed spent (a 0.5 s request at the start of the pass was refused with `quota_exceeded`, and the subscription call still lacks `user_read`), so the fourth pass generated nothing. It remade seven files from takes in hand, with the review's processing where processing could answer it:
- **`distant-1`** is the third pass's one 30 s distant take, in the review's own wording (prompt D, below): one take, no longer a ring of four. The generator returned it dual mono (left/right correlation 0.997), so its stereo is made from it (Distant loops, below), and its mono fold-down is the take itself.
- **`distant-2`** is a third-pass outer-reef take in the review's wording for `roar-2` (prompt H), one of the takes whose two channels the generator made different (correlation 0.54), moved to about a kilometre by the game's own air low-pass. - **`bubbles-1` and `bubbles-2`** are rebuilt from the second pass's takes with no layer (the foam layer carried `bubbles-1`'s 3.4 kHz whistle), a 12 dB/octave low-pass from 6 kHz and from 4 kHz in place of the brick-walls, `bubbles-2`'s ring picked again and its 0.5 s lulls lifted, and both wraps at the median level.
- **`lipJet-1`, `lipJet-2` and `lipRoller-1`** keep their takes. `lipJet-2` now starts 5 ms before its main impact and `lipRoller-1` is cut so its body arrives at 69 ms; their narrow lines are flattened (`lipJet-1`'s 651 Hz ring, `lipRoller-1`'s 8.8 kHz and 6.0 kHz whistles), and `lipJet-1`'s tick at 1.83 s is repaired.
- **Picks and gains.** `bubbles-1` is now the pick: without the layers `bubbles-2` is the darker of the two (its 1 kHz octave 43.8 dB under its loudest, against 17.2 dB, and a small speaker takes 12.0 dB off it, against 7.6 dB). `distant`'s gain rises from 1.32 to 1.86. Web Audio sends a mono file to both ears at full level, so a mono file measured at −23 LUFS plays 3 dB louder than a stereo one: the synthesised `distant` (−20.6 LUFS, mono) plays at −17.6 LUFS, and 1.86 brings the stereo files there. `lipJet`'s gain is 1.30 and `lipRoller`'s 1.04 (they were 1.32 and 0.99), from the repaired files' loudest 400 ms.

What changed in the engine (`src/audio` and the sound check), in the third pass and as the fifth pass corrected it:
- **Pools.** A candidate may list `variants`, more recordings of the same sound. `SoundBank` loads every file of the chosen candidate and hands them out in turn, shuffled, never the same one twice running; each shot is also detuned a little, by up to ±1 semitone for `lipJet` and `lipRoller` and ±1.5 semitones for `paddle` (the review's figures, provisional). Its level is never drawn at random: the third pass also varied it by up to ±1.5 dB (`lipJet`, `paddle`), and the fifth took that out, because the S1 plan has every level come from a measured quantity. No candidate is a pool yet.
- **Candidate level.** The four second candidates are matched to the first on the loudest 400 ms (EBU R128 momentary maximum, measured with ffmpeg): `lipJet-2` −1.28 dB (level 0.86), `lipRoller-2` +1.71 dB (1.22), `paddle-2` +3.19 dB (1.44), `plunge-2` +3.79 dB (1.55). The first two are set against the fourth pass's files (the third pass set 0.67 and 1.28). The sound check plays a candidate at its sound's gain times its level, so its A/B no longer favours the louder file.
- **Bubbles outside the muffle.** The bubbles are made under water, so they reach Master by a path at the Sea level that skips the 400 Hz low-pass; the muffle colours only what the camera hears through the surface. The pause menu still muffles them with the rest of the world (to 708 Hz), through a low-pass of their own that follows only the pause (the third pass's path skipped the pause menu's muffle too).
- **Air.** Every positional sound passes a 12 dB/octave low-pass whose cutoff falls with its distance from the camera: 18 kHz within 8 m, 4.5 kHz at 100 m, 2.4 kHz at 300 m, 1.2 kHz at 1 km, never below 900 Hz. The shape follows air absorption (ISO 9613-1: of the order of 5 dB/km at 1 kHz and 25 dB/km at 4 kHz in mild, humid air); the constants are provisional.
- **The context's rate.** The engine opens its AudioContext at 44.1 kHz, the recordings' own rate (`RECORDING_RATE`), and at the device's rate only where a browser refuses that one. At the device's rate (48 kHz on the machine these were measured on) Chrome's `decodeAudioData` resamples each file as a clip with two ends, which puts a burst at every loop's wrap that the file does not have (Wrap and loudness, below); at 44.1 kHz a loop plays its decoded samples as they are, and the browser resamples the context's output as one stream. A test checks that every shipped file is at that rate.
- **Chrome.** Chrome 154 (headless) decodes all fourteen files, as the fifth pass left them, to exactly their lengths at 44.1 kHz (`decodeAudioData`: 1,146,880 and 1,058,816 samples for the roars, 1,230,848 and 1,274,880 two-channel frames for the distant loops, 705,536 for the bubbles, 29,632–109,504 for the one-shots), within 2e-7 of ffmpeg's samples in every channel. Firefox was not available to try.

How they were made (nothing in the repo rebuilds them: the API has no seed, so a prompt gives a different take each time, and the raw takes are not committed):
- **Generation.** The first two passes used the ElevenLabs Sound Effects tool: 16-bit 44.1 kHz PCM out, loops as 5 s takes with the model's `loop` option on, one-shots of 1–3 s without it. 152 takes were generated in them (33, then 119) for the 41 takes used (37 new, and four first-pass takes behind the unchanged one-shots). Takes were set aside for a sample at or above −0.1 dBFS (no take used has one), the model changing state inside the take (a 25 dB cliff, a bright 8–14 kHz shelf, a long gap), a persistent whistle, no foam crackle where the prompt asked for it, or because a cleaner take for the same job was in hand. The third pass called the API directly (`POST /v1/sound-generation`, model `eleven_text_to_sound_v2`, `loop` on, `pcm_44100`): the tool has no model, influence or length control past 5 s. It generated 132 takes: 96 of 30 s for the roars over 14 wordings of the two roar prompts at `prompt_influence` 0.6 and 0.85, one 30 s distant take, 34 of 12 s to try wordings, and one 1.2 s lip crash. The API's cost header reads 301 credits for a 30 s take and 12 for 1.2 s (about 10 credits a second); the 132 takes cost 33,289. The key cannot read the plan's credit balance (the subscription call is refused for lack of the `user_read` permission). The fourth pass generated nothing: it used the third pass's distant take and one of its outer-reef takes, the second pass's bubble takes, and the first two passes' lip-crash takes.
- **The two channels.** The model returns two channels. Of the 96 roar takes of 30 s, 63 are dual mono (left/right correlation above 0.99) and 33 differ (correlation 0.32–0.96). The ones that differ come from two kinds of wording: an outer reef heard from a distance (25 of its 46 takes) and a "wide, relentless" unbroken surf zone (all 8); none of the other 42, which put the listener in the surf, does. The two roars are dual mono (0.996 and 0.998), folded to one channel by averaging. `distant-2`'s take is one that differs (0.54) and keeps both channels; `distant-1`'s is dual mono (0.997).
- **Roars.** Each is one 30 s take with a 1.0 s equal-power crossfade of its last second into its first, a 30 Hz high-pass (second order, run forwards and backwards: 24 dB/octave, −6 dB at 30 Hz) and a rotation that puts the wrap at the median level (the wrap sits +0.6 dB and +0.8 dB re the median of the 0.5 s levels, and −0.1 dB and −5.7 dB re the median of the 2–6 kHz against 100–500 Hz balance, the review's two checks of a wrap); there is no layer, no band-passed level ride and no spectrum shaping, and the gain is set only to −23.0 LUFS on the decoded file. The review's own prompts (a stereo recording standing waist-deep in heavy surf, and an outer reef 100 m away, both at influence 0.6) failed its texture gates in all twelve tries: 1–4 kHz kurtosis 3.05–3.42 and micro-variation 1.11–1.54 dB, and for the reef prompt a 63 Hz octave 12–35 dB under the loudest. So the roars come from rewordings (prompts F and G, above, at influence 0.85), picked by the review's gates, measured on the finished ring: 1–4 kHz kurtosis of at least 3.6 and micro-variation of at least 1.8 dB, the 63 Hz octave within 10 dB (`roar-1`) or 4 dB (`roar-2`) of the loudest octave. The gates alone do not make a roar. Of the 96 takes, 26 pass the two texture gates, and 16 of those have the 63 Hz octave within 10 dB; but eight of the 26 are sub-bass drones (spectral centroid 59–100 Hz; the wordings that asked for "a constant sub-bass rumble"), two are a 4 kHz hiss over a rumble, four (an "unbroken" wording) are sub-heavy with sparse splashes (centroid 224–701 Hz; the spectrogram of one shows a sub-bass slab with patches of splashes above 800 Hz and a hole between), and one changed scene three times in 26 s. The other eleven are shore-break takes, and only one of them, the pick for `roar-1`, has the weight (another reads 10.3 dB). The spectrograms found all that. `roar-2` is the closest take to its gates that looks and measures like surf: as a whole the take reads 1.76 dB of micro-variation against the gate's 1.8, and the 24 s ring cut from it reads 1.88 as the third pass encoded it and 1.87 as the fifth pass does (the 26 s ring first tried read 1.81, too close to the gate to trust).
- **Every bubbles bed take.** Folded to mono, DC removed, high-passed at 40 Hz (4th order, zero-phase: the output carries a lot of energy below 30 Hz) and low-passed at 12 dB/octave from 6 kHz (`bubbles-1`) or 4 kHz (`bubbles-2`), second order, one pass. The second pass had 4th-order zero-phase low-passes at 5 and 3.5 kHz there, the brick-walls the review found. The generator leaves a forest of narrow lines above 1 kHz, near multiples of 50–200 Hz, strongest in the quiet underwater takes (a comb score of +3.5 to +9.4 dB in the bubble takes used), and a dotted line near 2 kHz. Every bed take has each bin of its time-averaged spectrum between 600 Hz and 12 kHz that stands more than 3.5 dB over the median of its neighbours (±80 Hz) cut back to within 1.5 dB of it, by a zero-phase curve at the 2.7 Hz bin spacing.
- **Bubbles loops.** Four cores of 4.449 s are cut from four takes, at start offsets of 0–0.55 s, and the ring order and offsets are searched to make the four joins meet: level and spectrum over 1 s and 0.25 s windows, a lull inside the blend, an event beside it. The fourth pass picked `bubbles-2`'s four again from nine steady takes: the five rings that joined best all used deep takes only, and of those the one whose deepest 0.5 s window sat highest (4.9 dB under the median) was kept. Four frequency bands (split at 250, 1000 and 4000 Hz) each have their slow swell reduced by a level ride, its strength per band tuned in a closed loop towards 7.5 dB of 0.5 s-trend surge: 0, 0.52, 0.82 and 0 in `bubbles-1`, 0, 0.55, 0 and 0 in `bubbles-2`. The cores' average spectra are moved up to 6 dB towards each other, and join levels are matched band by band over 1 s and 0.25 s windows by a slow tilt across each core: at most 5.3 dB (`bubbles-1`) and 2.1 dB (`bubbles-2`) at either end of a core. The cores are joined end to start with 0.45 s equal-power crossfades, which makes a loop with no seam of its own.
- **No layers.** The second pass mixed a second ring into each bubbles loop at −20 dB (sea-foam crackle in `bubbles-1`, the brighter bubble takes in `bubbles-2`). The third review traced `bubbles-1`'s 3.4 kHz whistle to its layer, and the fourth pass dropped both. `bubbles-1` lost its fine fizz with it: its 4 kHz octave is 52.4 dB under its loudest, against 18.5 dB before.
- **Near the joins (bubbles).** Within 1 s of a bed blend, a lull (a 100 ms level more than 3 dB under its 1 s neighbourhood) is lifted by a smooth gain bump of at most 4.5 dB. Within 0.6 s of one, a peak more than 12.8 dB over its 300 ms RMS is pulled down towards that limit; the decoded files measure 11.2–13.7 dB within 0.5 s of a bed blend.
- **Lulls (`bubbles-2`).** Every 0.5 s window lying more than 3.6 dB under the loop's median 0.5 s level is lifted by a smooth broadband gain (a 0.5 s Hann bump, circular), at most 2.3 dB. No 0.5 s window is now more than 3.7 dB under the median, against the review's limit of 4 dB (the second pass's `bubbles-2` had a 7.2 dB hole).
- **Distant loops.** Each is one 30 s take made with `loop` on. The ring keeps 29.91 s of it, from 0.05 s in, and blends its last 2.0 s (`distant-1`) or 1.0 s (`distant-2`) into its first with an equal-power crossfade, which leaves rings of 27.91 s and 28.91 s, 1024 k + 960 samples, the length the plain encode needed (the fifth pass's encode adds 64 samples: AAC, below). `distant-2`'s blended windows meet within 1.9 dB in level and 2.3 dB in spectrum. `distant-1`'s take opens on its one big set, so its blend fades the quiet last 2 s into that swell (the two windows 12.6 dB apart), which makes a 2 s swell. Then a 30 Hz high-pass (second order, forwards and backwards, as for the roars) and the air of a distance, from the game's own model (`airCutoff`, a 12 dB/octave low-pass): 1.81 kHz for the 500 m that `distant-1`'s prompt names, and 1.23 kHz for the kilometre of the review's brief for `distant-2` (its take was asked for at 100 m), which puts their 4 kHz octaves 23.4 and 28.0 dB under the loudest. The bed plays unplaced, so the engine's air never reaches it; the file carries it instead. There is no layer and no level ride. `distant-1`'s stereo: the left channel is the take plus 0.577 (−4.8 dB) times a resynthesis of it, the right the take minus it. The resynthesis is the take's short-time spectrum (512-sample frames, a 64-sample hop, run around the loop so it stays seamless) with every bin's phase drawn at random: the same sound at the same moments, with fine structure of its own, uncorrelated with the take (0.008). That puts the left/right correlation at 0.51 and leaves the mono fold-down, (L + R)/2, exactly the take, 1.25 dB under a channel. Spreading the phase alone (an all-pass on each channel) was tried first: its fold-down had a fixed comb, which the review's line tracker counted (8 dB lines of 150 ms above 400 Hz: 55, against the take's 10).

- **AAC.** `afconvert -f m4af -d aac -b 128000`. It reports 2112 priming frames and pads the last frame; ffmpeg-based decoders keep that padding (620 extra samples on a 5 s test file), which would be a gap at a loop's wrap. A one-shot is therefore 1024 k + 960 samples long, which leaves no end padding: CoreAudio and ffmpeg both decode each file to exactly its source length, with samples that differ by less than 1e-6. They are not bit-identical: rounded to 16 bits, up to a few hundred samples a file differ by one step. The loops were made the same way until the fifth pass. The encoder treats a file as if silence came before its first sample and after its last, so the frames at both ends carry codec error the rest of the file does not, and at a loop's wrap the end's error meets the start's (The wrap, per channel, above). Each loop is now encoded as a circle:
  - its ring is stretched by 64 samples to a whole number of 1024-sample AAC frames, a band-limited resample around the loop (1.5 ms longer, 0.0009–0.0016 semitones lower): `roar-1` is 1024 × 1120 = 1,146,880 samples, `roar-2` 1024 × 1034 = 1,058,816, `distant-1` 1024 × 1202 = 1,230,848, `distant-2` 1024 × 1245 = 1,274,880 and the bubbles 1024 × 689 = 705,536;
  - afconvert encodes it with 16 frames of the loop's own tail before it and 16 of its head after it;
  - the file keeps one period of that packet stream (the packets whose frames cover the loop), after copies of the period's last two, and its packet table says 2048 priming frames, the loop's length and no remainder. CoreAudio's `AudioFile` API writes the file, laid out as afconvert's are (the audio from byte 8192, the gapless numbers in its iTunSMPB tag).

  Decoded, the file is one period of a steady, circular packet stream: its last frame and its first overlap like any two neighbouring frames (long, sine-window frames on both sides of the splice), so the wrap is an ordinary frame boundary. ffmpeg, CoreAudio and Chrome trim the 2048 priming frames as they trim afconvert's 2112, and decode each loop to exactly its length. Against its source, each loop's signal-to-codec-noise ratio is within 0.05 dB of the plain encode's or higher (by 4.5 and 6.7 dB in the bubbles). The two distant loops are stereo at 256 kb/s, the same 128 kb/s per channel as the mono files: in the fourth pass's trial the 128 kb/s stereo encode added faint tonal artefacts in their quiet treble (8 dB lines of 150 ms above 400 Hz: 17 in `distant-1` against 11 before encoding, 12 at 256 kb/s; 12, 10 and 10 in `distant-2`). The three repaired one-shots are mono at 192 kb/s for the same reason: at 128 kb/s the encode added 7–11 faint lines above 4 kHz (8 dB for 90 ms in 93 ms frames) to sounds that had none; at 192 kb/s 4–5 remain, and none at the review's 10 dB.

Known limits:
- **Nothing was heard.** The picks, the gains, the levels and the claims above come from spectrograms and numbers. - **Browsers.** Chrome 154 (at 44.1 and 48 kHz) and CoreAudio, Safari's decoder, were tried; Firefox was not. A browser that refuses a 44.1 kHz context opens the game's at the device's rate, and then resamples each loop as it decodes it, which puts a burst back at every wrap (Wrap and loudness).
- **The roars pump.** The review's gates are met, but not the review's "continuous, no single crash louder than the rest": `roar-1` has a 6.0 LU loudness range and `roar-2` 18.3 LU (the second pass's roars 3.3 and 3.6), with lulls of 15–16 dB. ## How to rebuild

- **Skies:** `npm run assets:skies`. It needs the network and macOS `sips`, and downloads into `scripts/assets/.cache/` (git-ignored).
- **Surfers:** `npm run assets:surfers`.
  - It needs Blender 5.2 at `/Applications/Blender.app` with the MPFB extension enabled (`Blender --online-mode --command extension install --enable mpfb`).
  - The first run downloads the 267 MB CC0 asset pack into `scripts/assets/.cache/` and installs it into MPFB's user data.
  - Rebuild some surfers only with `Blender --background --python scripts/assets/build_surfers.py -- surfer1 surfer2`, then `node scripts/assets/pack-surfers.mjs`.
  - The npm gltfpack has no texture codecs, so Blender writes the WebP textures and gltfpack compresses only the geometry.
- **Sounds:** nothing rebuilds them (see Sounds). To add or replace one, generate takes the same way, process them as described there, put the `.m4a` in `public/assets/audio/`, and list it in `sounds.json` and in this file; a one-shot may list `variants` (a pool played in turn) and a `level`. `npx vitest run src/audio` fails if the manifest lists a missing file (a pool's too), ships an unlisted one, or names a licence the game would drop.
